# Drivable Budget and Vendor Decision Guide V1

**Purpose:** Spend money only where it creates durable quality, proof, capacity, or distribution.  
**Status:** Planning ranges and decision rules—not purchase authorization or financial advice.

## 1. Spending Order

Prioritize in this order:

1. Product safety, privacy, reliability, and human-review capacity.
2. Domain, security, monitoring, and operational ownership.
3. Real product capture and founder media.
4. A small set of excellent editable creative masters.
5. Beta recruitment and organic validation.
6. Proof collection and rights/releases.
7. Small paid tests with stop-loss controls.
8. Public-scale production and media only after validated demand and capacity.

Do not spend heavily on reach before the product can safely absorb attention.

## 2. Build, Hire, or Buy Framework

### Build Internally When

- The asset depends on product truth, internal safety language, or rapid iteration.
- A reusable template can serve many channels.
- The team can verify quality without specialized equipment.
- The work is strategy, copy, brief, storyboard, asset organization, QA, or controlled graphic layout.

### Hire a Specialist When

- Real people/vehicles/locations need professional filming or photography.
- Audio quality, lighting, color, animation, or editing determines credibility.
- Trademark, advertising, privacy, marketplace, automotive safety, or jurisdiction-specific legal advice is needed.
- Accessibility, penetration testing, or security review needs independent expertise.
- A person must carry insurance or assume professional responsibility for real-world production.

### Buy/License When

- The asset has clear usage rights and is cheaper than recreating it.
- The tool solves an approved recurring workflow, not a one-time curiosity.
- The subscription can be owned, secured, audited, and canceled by the company.
- The license covers intended paid, social, press, app-store, and geographic use.

## 3. Vendor Categories and Evaluation

### Brand/Design

Ask for:

- Editable vector source and all rights/ownership terms.
- One-color, small-size, dark/light, and accessibility testing.
- Evidence that the designer can create a system, not only a logo image.
- Originality process and willingness to work through trademark findings.

Reject:

- Raster-only logos.
- Generic automotive/AI clip art.
- Unclear ownership, stock components that cannot be trademarked, or hidden recurring fees.

### Photo/Video

Ask for:

- Portfolio showing natural people and real environments.
- Crew/equipment, insurance, releases, location needs, deliverable matrix, raw-footage terms, captions, revisions, and storage.
- Ability to follow vehicle and phone safety rules.
- Rights covering organic, paid, press, website, app-store, and future edits.

Reject:

- Unsafe driving/phone concepts.
- Talent or music without clear rights.
- Deliverables limited to one crop or compressed platform downloads.

### Performance Marketing

Ask for:

- Who owns ad accounts, pixels, audiences, landing pages, and creative.
- Experience optimizing for qualified outcomes—not clicks alone.
- Budget authority, daily monitoring, incident escalation, transparency, and cancellation terms.
- How they handle regulated/sensitive audience policies and customer data.

Reject:

- Agency-owned accounts the company cannot recover.
- Guaranteed results.
- Pressure to spend before conversion, support, privacy, and attribution are ready.
- Undisclosed markups or broad permission to create claims.

### Creator/Influencer

Ask for:

- Audience evidence, prior automotive/consumer content, disclosure practices, usage/whitelisting rights, edits, exclusivity, and performance reporting.
- Exact script boundaries and permission to correct misleading claims.

Reject:

- Fake testimonials, undisclosed relationships, guaranteed reach/sales, unsafe stunts, or creator ownership of the only master footage.

## 5. Staged Budget Envelope Template

The founder should set actual amounts only after obtaining quotes and confirming available capital.

| Stage | Primary spending | Maximum approved envelope | Owner | Stop condition |
|---|---|---:|---|---|
| Pre-beta | Security, domain, product readiness, limited brand/export work | Not authorized | Founder approval required | Product gate not advancing |
| Closed beta | Review/support, controlled filming, beta recruitment | Not authorized | Founder approval required | Capacity or safety threshold exceeded |
| Proof | Permissioned stories, product capture, organic tests | Not authorized | Founder approval required | Proof/permission insufficient |
| Paid validation | Small media test and landing optimization | Not authorized | Founder approval required | Stop-loss metric reached |
| Public launch | Final production, press, approved media | Not authorized | Founder approval required | Go/no-go fails |
| Scale | Incremental winning channels/creative | Not authorized | Founder approval required | Quality or capacity deteriorates |

## 6. Paid Test Budget Rules

- Use money the company can afford to lose without compromising product safety or operations.
- Set platform-level daily and total limits before launch.
- Test one objective and one primary product at a time.
- Do not increase budget because click-through rate is high if application quality is poor.
- Require a named person who can pause campaigns immediately.
- Treat platform-reported attribution as directional until independently checked.
- Separate creative testing from audience testing when possible so conclusions remain interpretable.
- Do not let a vendor raise spend without written approval.

## 7. Avoidable Early Purchases

- Large annual software contracts before workflow fit is proven.
- Premium camera equipment that a contractor already supplies.
- Large stock libraries before visual direction is final.
- Paid followers, engagement, reviews, leads, or customer lists.
- Broad influencer retainers without a tested brief.
- Multiple overlapping design/project-management/analytics tools.
- Trademark merchandise before mark clearance.
- Mass printed collateral before product, pricing, URLs, and QR destinations are stable.

## 8. Rights and Ownership Checklist

Before paying a final invoice:

- [ ] Company name on contract and invoice is correct.
- [ ] Exact deliverables and source files are received.
- [ ] Ownership/license covers intended channels, paid use, geography, duration, edits, and archive.
- [ ] Talent, property, vehicle, photographer, music, font, stock, and third-party rights are documented.
- [ ] No prohibited confidential/customer data is in deliverables.
- [ ] Vendor access is removed or reduced after handoff.
- [ ] Assets, contracts, licenses, and invoices are stored in company-controlled systems.
- [ ] Renewal/cancellation dates are recorded.

## 9. Founder Purchase Decision

Before approving any meaningful spend, answer:

1. What launch gate does this purchase help pass?
2. Can existing assets or a smaller test answer the same question?
3. Who owns the account and output?
4. What recurring obligation does this create?
5. What happens if the product or launch date changes?
6. Can the asset be reused and edited?
7. Are rights, privacy, safety, and claims responsibilities explicit?
8. What measurable result will determine whether to continue?

If the answer to the first or eighth question is unclear, do not purchase yet.
