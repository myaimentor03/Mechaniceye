# Drivable Marketing Claims and Creative Review V1

**Purpose:** Prevent misleading, unsafe, unsupported, or inconsistent marketing before any asset is approved or published.

## 1. Claim Classes

### Class A — Product-Behavior Claims

These describe a feature that can be directly demonstrated in the current product.

Examples:

- “Organize vehicle details, symptoms, and evidence.”
- “See possible causes and missing information.”
- “Prepare questions for a mechanic.”

Approval requires:

- The feature works in the advertised customer flow.
- The landing page and product use the same terminology.
- A reviewer can reproduce the behavior.

### Class B — Boundary and Process Claims

These describe how Drivable handles uncertainty, review, evidence, or safety.

Examples:

- “Missing evidence should lower confidence.”
- “Higher-risk cases may require human review.”
- “Drivable does not provide a safety clearance.”

Approval requires:

- Product behavior matches the statement.
- Exceptions and availability are not hidden.
- Human review is not promised unless staffing and turnaround are defined.

### Class C — Performance and Outcome Claims

These claim or imply accuracy, savings, time reduction, successful repairs, successful purchases/sales, customer satisfaction, or comparative performance.

Examples:

- “Save hundreds on repairs.”
- “90% accurate.”
- “Avoid unnecessary repairs.”
- “Sell your vehicle faster.”

Status: **blocked until substantiated and formally approved.**

Approval requires a claim substantiation record defining the exact wording, metric, population, time period, sample size, method, exclusions, confidence/uncertainty, owner, expiry date, and supporting source.

### Class D — Third-Party and Social-Proof Claims

These include testimonials, star ratings, media quotes, partner logos, mechanic credentials, customer counts, and creator endorsements.

Status: **blocked until permission and factual scope are documented.**

An endorsement must disclose material relationships when required. A customer quote cannot be edited into a stronger result than the customer reported.

## 2. Approved Core Claims

Approved only when the corresponding live flow supports them:

| Message | Safe default wording | Required context |
|---|---|---|
| Main promise | “Get a clearer next move for a confusing vehicle decision.” | Do not imply certainty or a guaranteed outcome. |
| Evidence organization | “Organize the details and evidence you provide.” | Evidence remains user-provided or attributed. |
| Possible causes | “See up to three possible causes when the evidence supports them.” | Causes are not confirmed diagnoses. |
| Confidence | “See how confident the current inference is—and what could change it.” | Do not use uncalibrated percentages. |
| Missing information | “See what information is missing before a higher-stakes decision.” | Do not imply all missing information can be collected remotely. |
| Mechanic conversation | “Prepare a clearer symptom summary and questions for a shop.” | Drivable does not direct or guarantee a repair. |
| Buyer Check | “Review seller claims, submitted evidence, gaps, and questions before you spend more time or money.” | Not an inspection or verification service. |
| Seller Check | “Organize seller-reported details, observed evidence, known issues, and unanswered questions.” | Seller remains responsible for truthful disclosure. |
| Human review | “Some higher-risk or materially uncertain cases may require human review.” | Availability and scope must be disclosed. |

## 3. Prohibited or Restricted Language

### Always Prohibited Without a Separately Verified Narrow Scope

- “Safe to drive.”
- “Certified vehicle,” “Drivable certified,” or “mechanic approved.”
- “Confirmed diagnosis” from remote AI analysis.
- “Guaranteed repair,” “guaranteed fix,” or “guaranteed sale.”
- “No risk,” “nothing to worry about,” or “you do not need a mechanic.”
- “Clean title,” “one owner,” “accident free,” or “mileage verified” when based on seller input alone.
- “AI inspection” when no physical inspection occurred.

### Proof-Dependent and Currently Blocked

- Accuracy percentages.
- Savings or repair-avoidance amounts.
- Faster selling or higher selling-price claims.
- Number of mechanics available or response-time claims.
- Customer counts, ratings, or satisfaction statistics.
- “Most trusted,” “best,” “leading,” or superiority comparisons.
- “Catches problems mechanics miss” or anti-mechanic positioning.

## 4. Seller-Claim Firewall for Marketing

Marketing and listing creative must preserve the product's evidence integrity:

1. A seller statement remains “seller-reported” until independently verified.
2. Absence of contradicting evidence is not verification.
3. A receipt may support that work was invoiced; it does not prove present condition or successful repair.
4. AI rewriting cannot remove attribution or qualifiers.
5. Observed evidence and seller claims must remain visibly distinct.
6. Contradictions must not be omitted to make a story or listing more persuasive.

Safe example:

> Seller reports recent brake work. A dated invoice was provided. Current brake condition has not been independently inspected.

Unsafe example:

> New brakes. Ready to drive.

## 5. Mandatory Review Checklist

Every customer-facing asset must pass all applicable checks.

### Product Truth

- [ ] The advertised feature exists in the intended customer flow.
- [ ] The CTA leads to the promised experience.
- [ ] Availability, price, turnaround, geography, and eligibility are accurate.
- [ ] Demo screens are current and test/sample data is visibly labeled.

### Claims

- [ ] Every factual or outcome claim has an approved source.
- [ ] Possible causes are not called diagnoses.
- [ ] No safety clearance, guarantee, or unsupported comparison is implied.
- [ ] Seller claims remain attributed and unverified where appropriate.
- [ ] Qualifiers are prominent enough to be noticed and understood.

### Visual Safety and Authenticity

- [ ] No driver is recording, reading, typing, or operating a phone while moving.
- [ ] No person is shown reaching near hot/moving parts or under an unsupported vehicle.
- [ ] Warning lights, dashboards, tools, vehicle geometry, license plates, and hands are plausible.
- [ ] Generated people are not represented as real customers, staff, mechanics, or testimonials.
- [ ] No protected logo, artwork, music, vehicle photo, or private evidence is used without rights.

### Privacy and Permissions

- [ ] Faces, voices, vehicles, plates, documents, and customer evidence have appropriate permission or are safely anonymized.
- [ ] Testimonial/media releases and licenses are stored with the asset record.
- [ ] Sensitive information is not visible in screens, paperwork, VINs, metadata, or reflections.
- [ ] Tracking and lead collection follow the approved consent/privacy design.

### Accessibility and Quality

- [ ] Text contrast and size meet the chosen accessibility standard.
- [ ] Meaning does not rely on color alone.
- [ ] Video has accurate captions; essential visuals have spoken or textual context.
- [ ] Mobile safe areas, crop behavior, spelling, audio levels, and link behavior are verified.
- [ ] The asset still makes sense when viewed without sound.

## 6. Approval States

| State | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Draft | Work in progress; not shareable as public marketing. |
| Product review | Feature, offer, CTA, and factual behavior are being checked. |
| Safety/claims review | Language, imagery, substantiation, and boundaries are being checked. |
| Rights/privacy review | Releases, licenses, consent, and sensitive data are being checked. |
| Approved | Exact version is cleared for stated channels and dates. |
| Channel-ready | Correct export, destination, tracking, caption, alt text, and schedule are attached. |
| Published | Live placement and publication time are recorded. |
| Retired | Must not be reused; reason and replacement are recorded. |

Approval applies to an exact version. Editing a headline, crop, voiceover, screenshot, claim, CTA, or testimonial after approval returns the asset to review.

## 7. Paid Advertising Go/No-Go Gate

Paid traffic remains **no-go** until all are true:

- The advertised flow is stable and has passed end-to-end testing.
- Landing pages, analytics, conversion events, privacy/consent, and UTM conventions are ready.
- Customer support and human review capacity have defined limits.
- The campaign has an owner, budget cap, daily monitoring, stop-loss condition, and refund/correction process.
- Every ad and landing page passes this checklist.
- Beta evidence supports the chosen message without requiring blocked performance claims.
