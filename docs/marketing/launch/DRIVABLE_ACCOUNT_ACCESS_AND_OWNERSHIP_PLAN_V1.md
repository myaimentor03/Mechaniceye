# Drivable Account Access and Ownership Plan V1

**Status:** Planning document; do not open or purchase every account immediately.  
**Brand:** Drivable by Mechanic's Eye  
**Principle:** The company/founder owns critical accounts. Workers receive the narrowest role needed. Passwords, recovery codes, API keys, payment details, and identity documents are never pasted into project tasks.

## 1. What to Open Now

Only create accounts when there is an approved owner, purpose, recovery plan, and near-term use.

### Pre-Beta Essentials

| Account/system | Purpose | Founder action | Gate before creation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Company-controlled email domain | Ownership, recovery, public contact, role inboxes | Purchase/configure through a reputable provider | Confirm company/domain naming and legal owner |
| Password manager | Secure shared access and recovery | Create company vault and emergency recovery | Choose owner and backup administrator |
| Primary domain/DNS | Website and email control | Register under company/founder account | Domain/trademark review |
| Website hosting/deployment | Beta landing and app | Confirm existing host ownership | Deployment/security owner identified |
| Analytics | Privacy-aware beta funnel measurement | Create property under company account | Event plan and privacy/consent approved |
| Error/uptime monitoring | Detect broken intake and launch incidents | Create under company account | Technical owner and alert destination |
| Beta application/intake | Recruit controlled testers | Use the approved app/form flow | Safety screen, consent, routing, and support tested |
| Press/contact inbox | Media and partnership communication | Create role address | Response owner and spam/security process |

### Do Not Open Yet Unless a Specific Work Package Requires It

- Large paid-advertising accounts with active billing.
- Creator/influencer marketplaces.
- Mass email or SMS plans sized for public launch.
- Affiliate platforms.
- Marketplace payment/escrow accounts.
- Mechanic background-check or credential vendors.
- Expensive stock-media, video, music, or design subscriptions.
- App-store developer accounts before the distribution decision is confirmed.

Opening an account creates security, billing, legal, privacy, and maintenance obligations. More accounts do not make the launch more prepared.

## 2. Recommended Role Addresses

Create only when the domain is finalized:

- `support@...` — customer support and corrections.
- `safety@...` — safety concerns and adverse events.
- `privacy@...` — privacy rights and data questions.
- `press@...` — media requests.
- `partners@...` — mechanic, shop, and organization inquiries.
- `billing@...` — payment/refund matters when payments exist.
- `security@...` — responsible security reports.

Do not use the founder's personal email as the permanent owner/contact for every system. Role inboxes need named human owners and backup coverage.

## 3. Ownership Rules

- Register domains, hosting, analytics, advertising, email, and payment accounts to the company/founder—not a freelancer or agency.
- Use a company-controlled email address for the primary owner.
- Maintain at least two authorized administrators for critical systems where possible.
- Require multi-factor authentication. Prefer authenticator or hardware security keys over SMS when supported.
- Store recovery codes in the company password manager and one protected offline recovery location.
- Never share one login among many people when role-based access is available.
- Review access quarterly and immediately after a worker, vendor, or agency relationship ends.
- Record who can publish, spend, export customer data, change billing, change DNS, or add users.

## 4. Access Classes

| Class | Capability | Examples | Approval |
|---|---|---|---|
| Owner | Billing, recovery, users, deletion, legal acceptance | Founder/company officer | Human-controlled only |
| Administrator | Configuration and role management | Trusted technical/operations lead | Explicit founder approval |
| Publisher | Publish approved assets; no billing/user control | Marketing operator | Campaign-specific |
| Analyst | Read reports and performance data | Product/marketing analyst | Least-data scope |
| Contributor | Draft assets/configuration without publishing | Designer/copy worker | Default worker access |
| Reviewer | Approve claims/safety/privacy within scope | Qualified reviewer | Qualification documented |

Codex or another worker should normally receive no persistent credentials. Prefer temporary, scoped connections and human confirmation for external publishing or account changes.

## 5. Secret and Sensitive-Data Rules

Never paste into chat or commit to the repository:

- Passwords or password-reset links.
- API, OAuth, webhook-signing, or encryption keys.
- Multi-factor codes or recovery codes.
- Payment-card or bank information.
- Government identity documents.
- Raw customer exports, VIN lists, private media, or signed releases.
- Advertising custom-audience files.

Safe workflow:

1. The operator identifies the exact service, access level, and purpose.
2. The founder opens or connects the account through the service's official interface.
3. Secrets are stored in the service/host secret manager or company password manager.
4. The task receives only confirmation and non-sensitive identifiers needed for work.
5. Access is tested without displaying the secret.

## 6. Channel Account Sequence

### Stage 0 — Reserve and Secure

After naming/domain review:

- Reserve consistent brand handles on the few priority networks.
- Add approved avatar/description only after the brand lockup passes visual review.
- Enable MFA and store recovery ownership.
- Do not publish empty hype or unavailable-feature claims merely to make the accounts look active.

### Stage 1 — Closed Beta

- Use founder outreach, email, and permission-based community posts.
- Activate only the social channels the team can monitor and answer responsibly.
- Do not enable paid billing until the paid-test gate passes.

### Stage 2 — Proof and Organic Testing

- Publish approved educational series and real product demonstrations.
- Add video platforms once repeatable filming/editing capacity exists.
- Test content concepts organically before paying to amplify them.

### Stage 3 — Paid/Public Launch

- Connect billing only to approved advertising accounts.
- Apply strict spending limits, named budget owner, and stop-loss rules.
- Grant agencies/contractors partner access—not account ownership or shared passwords.

## 7. Account Register Requirement

No launch-channel, advertising, analytics, email, SMS, app-store, creator, press, or vendor account is approved in this repository. The future restricted register must record the service, official URL, purpose, legal/company owner, primary and backup administrators, billing owner, MFA method, recovery-record location, connected systems, data categories, renewal/cost, access-review date, and deletion/export procedure. Secrets and recovery codes must never be stored in this public documentation area.

## 8. Founder Copy/Paste Checklist for a Future Account Request

When the operator asks you to create or connect a service, confirm:

- [ ] I am on the service's official website or verified app.
- [ ] I understand why Drivable needs this service now.
- [ ] The account is owned by the company/founder, not a worker.
- [ ] The recovery email and phone are controlled and current.
- [ ] MFA is enabled and recovery codes are stored securely.
- [ ] Billing limits and renewal terms are understood.
- [ ] I did not paste credentials or sensitive data into a task.
- [ ] Worker access is role-based and no broader than necessary.
- [ ] The service's data handling fits the approved privacy plan.

## 9. When the Operator Must Stop and Ask

The operator must request founder action or approval immediately before:

- Creating or accepting the final terms for an external account.
- Connecting a live account that exposes private customer/business data.
- Granting permissions or adding a user.
- Creating persistent credentials.
- Entering payment information or confirming a purchase.
- Publishing, sending, or submitting content as Drivable.
- Uploading customer lists, releases, identifiable media, or other sensitive data.
- Changing domain, email, security, billing, or public account settings.

The request should state the exact action, service, data affected, external-state change, and narrowest useful permission.
