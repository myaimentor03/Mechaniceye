# Drivable Video Production Kit V1

**Brand:** Drivable by Mechanic's Eye  
**Priority products:** Drivable Check and Buyer Check  
**Production principle:** Real founder, real parked vehicles, real approved product capture, and permissioned beta evidence are preferred for hero media. Generated imagery may support secondary scenes but must not impersonate real customers, mechanics, outcomes, or product behavior.

## 2. Production Standards

### Required

- Film vehicles fully parked unless professional closed-course footage is separately planned.
- The driver never records, types, reads, or operates the product while driving.
- Sensitive app data, names, VINs, plates, addresses, receipts, and notifications use approved demo data or are obscured in camera.
- Mechanics, shops, customers, vehicles, and locations have written releases where required.
- Any statement about a result has a substantiation and permission record.
- Product capture is recorded from a stable approved beta build with sample/demo labeling when applicable.
- Every master has captions, transcript, alt/description copy, music/license records, and a clean version without platform text overlays.

### Avoid

- Staged emergencies, panic, smoke, fire, unsafe roadside behavior, or recreating a dangerous symptom.
- “AI vision” graphics, holograms, robots, diagnostic certainty, or fake scanning effects.
- Anti-mechanic caricatures, confrontation, secret recording, or “caught cheating” scenes.
- Luxury-only vehicles, immaculate generic stock imagery, or implausible repair environments.
- Hands holding phones in ways that could imply driving distraction.

## 3. Brand Hero Film — 60 Seconds

**Working title:** The Moment Before the Next Decision  
**Stage:** Shoot only after beta yields permissioned product behavior and at least one appropriately scoped story.  
**Primary CTA:** Start with Drivable  
**Core promise:** See what the evidence supports, what is still unknown, and what to ask next.

### Storyboard and Script

| Time | Picture | Audio/voiceover | On-screen text |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 0–5s | Close details of an everyday parked car: dashboard photographed while parked, tire wear, owner listening beside car. | “Vehicle decisions usually arrive before certainty does.” | `Something changed.` |
| 5–12s | Owner at a table with phone, OBD reader, abstract notes, and a repair document with sensitive details obscured. | “A light. A sound. An estimate. A used-car listing that leaves out the questions you need answered.” | None |
| 12–20s | Real Drivable Check intake: goal, symptoms, timing, safe evidence. | “Drivable starts with what happened—and the evidence you can gather safely.” | `Symptoms • timing • evidence` |
| 20–29s | Report structure: safety, possible causes, confidence, missing evidence. Do not show a confirmed diagnosis. | “It organizes possible causes, confidence, safety signals, and what is still missing.” | `Possible. Supported. Still unknown.` |
| 29–37s | Owner and technician discussing a concise handoff beside a parked vehicle. | “So the next conversation can start with a clearer story and better questions.” | `A better mechanic handoff` |
| 37–45s | Buyer examining a parked used car; Buyer Check claim/evidence/unknown interface. | “And when you are considering a used car, Buyer Check keeps claims, observations, risks, and unknowns distinct.” | `Seller-reported ≠ observed` |
| 45–53s | Founder speaking in a real garage/driveway; brief beta participant moment only with release. | “It is not a remote inspection or safety clearance. It is a clearer way to decide what should happen next.” | `Evidence before certainty.` |
| 53–60s | Dark brand card and product screens. | “Drivable by Mechanic's Eye. Start with what happened.” | `Drivable by Mechanic's Eye` / `Start with what happened.` / `getdrivable.com` |

### Mandatory End-Card Boundary

`Informational guidance based on information provided. Not a confirmed diagnosis, physical inspection, emergency service, or safety clearance.`

### Proof Dependency

Do not include a customer result, savings, successful repair, successful purchase, or satisfaction statement until the exact story is permissioned and substantiated. The film works without outcome claims.

## 4. Founder Story — 2 to 3 Minutes

### Narrative Structure

1. **The lived problem:** why vehicle decisions are confusing and what the founder observed.
2. **The product belief:** uncertainty should be made visible rather than hidden.
3. **How Drivable works:** evidence, possible causes, confidence, missing information, better questions.
4. **Respect for hands-on expertise:** Drivable prepares and routes; it does not replace qualified inspection.
5. **Buyer transparency:** claims and observations should remain distinct.
6. **Why beta matters:** earn trust through testing, review, and real outcomes before scaling.
7. **Invitation:** appropriate non-emergency testers can help build it responsibly.

### Founder Interview Questions

- What vehicle experience first made you think ordinary people need a clearer way to organize the situation?
- What do customers commonly struggle to explain to a mechanic?
- Why is “possible cause” more honest than pretending every remote answer is a diagnosis?
- What should a customer be able to see about confidence and missing evidence?
- What does a respectful customer–mechanic conversation look like?
- Why should seller claims and observed evidence remain separate?
- What must Drivable prove during beta before you are comfortable launching broadly?
- What should someone never use Drivable for?
- What do you want a customer to feel after using Drivable?

### Founder Language Guardrails

Use:

- “We are building…”
- “The beta is designed to test…”
- “Possible causes based on the information provided…”
- “We want to help people ask better questions…”
- “Some situations require hands-on inspection or immediate help.”

Avoid:

- “It always knows.”
- “It diagnoses anything.”
- “It stops mechanics from ripping people off.”
- “Only honest cars and sellers.”
- “We vet every mechanic” before the program operates.
- “This will save everyone money.”

## 5. Product Walkthrough — 90 Seconds

### Capture Requirements

- Use a dedicated demo account and synthetic vehicle/case information.
- Show a non-emergency scenario approved by the safety reviewer.
- Clearly label sample/mock content if the output is not a real approved report.
- Record at high resolution with the final browser/device scale and no personal notifications.
- Use pointer movement and zoom sparingly; edit for clarity rather than speed.

### Chapter Script

**0–10 seconds — Choose the decision**  
“Start with what you are trying to decide: a current vehicle concern or a used-car question.”

**10–27 seconds — Describe what happened**  
“Add the vehicle, symptom, timing, warning indicators, recent work, and what changed.”

**27–41 seconds — Add supported information safely**  
“Add the written details and user-entered vehicle information the current release supports. Never take a safety risk to gather more.”

**41–63 seconds — Understand the output**  
“Drivable organizes possible causes when supported, confidence, conflicting or missing evidence, and the next useful checks.”

**63–78 seconds — Prepare the next conversation**  
“Use the mechanic handoff or Buyer Check questions to make the next conversation more specific.”

**78–90 seconds — Boundary and CTA**  
“Drivable is informational guidance, not a physical inspection, confirmed diagnosis, emergency service, or safety clearance. Learn more at getdrivable.com.”

## 6. Drivable Check Vertical Video Set

### DC-01 — Warning Light Is Not the Whole Story

**Hook:** “A warning light is information—not the whole answer.”  
**Visual:** Parked vehicle, written dashboard-light selection, timing question, user-entered OBD status, missing-information card.  
**CTA:** Start with what happened.  
**Boundary:** Critical warnings may require stopping and qualified help.

### DC-02 — Hard-to-Explain Noise

**Hook:** “You do not need the right car word. Start with when it happens.”  
**Visual:** Owner safely listening beside parked car; simple timing conditions.  
**CTA:** Build a clearer symptom story.  
**Boundary:** Do not drive or recreate a symptom to record it.

### DC-03 — Repair Estimate

**Hook:** “Before approving parts, ask what the evidence supports.”  
**Visual:** Estimate fields, tests performed, urgent/can-wait questions.  
**CTA:** Prepare better questions.  
**Boundary:** No savings or mechanic-misconduct claim.

### DC-04 — Possible Cause Is Not Diagnosis

**Hook:** “One symptom can point to more than one cause.”  
**Visual:** Three cause cards with different support and missing checks.  
**CTA:** See how confidence works.

### DC-05 — Better Mechanic Handoff

**Hook:** “Give the shop a clearer starting point.”  
**Visual:** Timeline, evidence, questions, respectful shop conversation.  
**CTA:** Build my mechanic summary.  
**Boundary:** No outcome or provider guarantee.

### DC-06 — Not Enough Information

**Hook:** “Sometimes the responsible answer is: we need more evidence.”  
**Visual:** Missing-information request and human-review route.  
**CTA:** See what would change the decision.

## 7. Buyer Check Vertical Video Set

### BC-01 — Seller Says

**Hook:** “Seller says” is a source—not proof.  
**Visual:** Seller-reported, document-supported, observed, unknown.  
**CTA:** Review the listing.

### BC-02 — Before the Drive

**Hook:** “Before you drive two hours, check what the listing leaves unanswered.”  
**Visual:** Missing photos, records, VIN/title questions, inspection access.  
**CTA:** Start Buyer Check.  
**Boundary:** Not title or condition verification.

### BC-03 — Supported Positive

**Hook:** “A positive detail still needs a source and a date.”  
**Visual:** Recent tire receipt versus current tire observation.  
**CTA:** See what the evidence actually supports.

### BC-04 — Walk-Away Conditions

**Hook:** “A refused inspection is information.”  
**Visual:** Inspection offered/completed/declined labels without inferring motive.  
**CTA:** Prepare your questions.  
**Boundary:** Buyer makes the final purchase decision.

## 8. Production-Day Shot List

### Founder

- Medium direct-to-camera interview.
- Walking/talking beside a safely parked everyday car.
- Reviewing product notes at a table.
- Using the real beta app while stationary.
- Quiet listening/reaction shots for edit transitions.

### Drivable Check

- Exterior vehicle establishing shots.
- Parked-vehicle dashboard-light selection shown in the released interface; do not imply photo analysis.
- OBD reader connection with engine state controlled and safety-reviewed.
- Non-sensitive repair document.
- Abstract symptom timeline.
- App intake and report screen capture.

### Buyer Check

- Buyer observing tires, body panels, dashboard, and provided records in a safe location.
- Phone checklist use while stationary.
- Seller claim versus observed evidence UI.
- Independent inspection conversation if a real provider agrees and releases are signed.

### Mechanic Handoff

- Respectful customer/technician conversation.
- Technician reading a concise handoff.
- Requested-check list with no diagnosis/result fabricated.
- Shop exterior/interior without visible third-party branding unless permission is documented.

### Brand Texture

- Keys on table.
- Phone and evidence details.
- Natural vehicle wear.
- Garage/driveway environmental footage.
- Dark/light brand surfaces for transitions.

## 9. Minimum Production Setup

Highest quality does not require a giant crew. A disciplined small setup can work:

- Camera capable of clean 4K 10-bit capture or a recent phone with manual exposure/white-balance control.
- Stable tripod and compact gimbal only where useful.
- Two wireless lavalier microphones plus a backup recorder.
- One soft key light/reflector and controlled practical lighting.
- Neutral density filter for outdoor consistency where appropriate.
- Slate/timecode or disciplined clip naming.
- Dedicated person responsible for safety, releases, continuity, and sensitive-data checks.

Do not purchase equipment before deciding whether a local professional can supply it more economically.

## 11. Video Approval Checklist

- [ ] Product behavior matches repository readiness and deployed beta behavior.
- [ ] No unsafe phone/vehicle/repair behavior is shown.
- [ ] Demo/test content is labeled appropriately.
- [ ] Every person, location, vehicle, logo, screen, and document has permission or is safely obscured.
- [ ] No unsupported result, savings, accuracy, vetting, or honesty claim is stated or implied.
- [ ] Seller claims remain attributed.
- [ ] Captions and transcript are accurate.
- [ ] Dialogue is intelligible on phone speakers; music does not mask safety language.
- [ ] Essential meaning works without sound.
- [ ] CTA destination and product label are accurate.
- [ ] All aspect ratios are manually reframed, not blindly center-cropped.
