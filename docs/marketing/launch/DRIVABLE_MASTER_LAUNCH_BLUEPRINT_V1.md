# Drivable Master Launch Blueprint V1

**Status:** Active pre-beta foundation  
**Owner:** Drivable launch program  
**Last updated:** 2026-08-24

## 1. The Assignment

Build Drivable into a trusted, recognizable consumer product and prepare a large public launch after beta proves the product can deliver safely, consistently, and usefully.

This is not a request to make a large pile of graphics. It is a coordinated system of positioning, proof, creative production, distribution, measurement, customer support, and safety controls.

## 2. What “Massive Launch” Should Mean

A massive launch is not maximum spend on day one. It is maximum prepared impact:

- One memorable product promise.
- A clear reason to act now.
- Enough credible creative variants for every priority channel.
- Demonstrable product behavior and real beta evidence.
- A reliable path from advertisement to useful outcome.
- Support and review capacity that can handle demand.
- Measurement that identifies what works before budget expands.
- Stop conditions that protect customers and the company.

The public launch should feel large because it is coordinated, not because it is reckless.

## 3. Brand Architecture Recommendation

Use **Drivable by Mechanic's Eye** as the confirmed master brand. **Drivable** is the dominant consumer-facing wordmark and spoken product name; **by Mechanic's Eye** is the endorsement line.

Why:

- “Drivable” is shorter and better aligned with the decision promise.
- The repository already uses Drivable across Buyer Check, ClearSale, Mechanic Match, and reports.
- “Mechanic's Eye” adds continuity and parent-brand trust without competing in every headline.

The founder confirmed this brand relationship on 2026-08-24. Trademark, domain, and legal-entity review are still required before material identity or advertising spend.

Creative should keep the endorsement as an editable vector/text element instead of baking it into photography. This preserves layout flexibility without weakening the confirmed brand.

## 4. Recommended Positioning

### Category

Vehicle decision support.

### Core Promise

**A clearer next move for a confusing vehicle decision.**

### Supporting Explanation

Drivable organizes the details and evidence you provide into possible causes, confidence, missing information, safety flags, and practical questions for what comes next.

### Trust Difference

Drivable is designed to show uncertainty instead of hiding it. It does not claim a remote inspection, confirmed diagnosis, or safety clearance.

### Initial Launch Wedge

Lead with a situation almost every driver recognizes:

> Something changed with the car. Before you panic, approve parts, chase a listing, or give up on it, get the situation organized.

Do not lead the first mass campaign with every product module at once. The main campaign should establish Drivable Check. Buyer Check, Seller Check/ClearSale, and Mechanic Match become scenario-specific follow-on campaigns.

## 5. Priority Audiences

### Audience A — Worried Everyday Owner

- Trigger: warning light, sound, vibration, no-start, or changed behavior.
- Emotional state: uncertainty, fear of cost, fear of being stranded or misled.
- Desired result: understand urgency and speak to a shop more clearly.
- Best entry product: Drivable Check.
- Avoid: fear exploitation or implying that remote analysis can clear the vehicle for driving.

### Audience B — Repair-Decision Owner

- Trigger: estimate, repeated repair, unclear recommendation, older vehicle.
- Desired result: understand what supports the repair path and what to ask before approving work.
- Best entry product: Full Decision Report / mechanic handoff.
- Avoid: accusing shops of dishonesty or promising savings.

### Audience C — Used-Car Buyer

- Trigger: attractive private-party listing, distant vehicle, incomplete seller story.
- Desired result: identify missing proof, questions, red flags, and inspection priorities.
- Best entry product: Buyer Check.
- Avoid: implying title, seller, mileage, or vehicle verification.

### Audience D — As-Is Seller

- Trigger: problem vehicle, too costly to fix, unclear how to explain condition.
- Desired result: prepare a clearer, more transparent listing.
- Best entry product: Seller Check / ClearSale.
- Avoid: hiding defects, “certification,” or laundering seller claims through AI prose.

### Audience E — Mechanic-Conversation Planner

- Trigger: poor vocabulary, anxiety about calling a shop, scattered symptoms.
- Desired result: a concise evidence-based handoff.
- Best entry product: mechanic script / Mechanic Match.
- Avoid: implying provider endorsement, price, availability, or outcome guarantees.

## 6. Message Pillars

### Pillar 1 — Start With What Happened

Plain-language details are useful. The customer does not need to know automotive terminology.

### Pillar 2 — Evidence Before Certainty

Timing, warning lights, written observations, user-entered OBD information, recent work, and history can clarify the path. Missing information should lower confidence, not produce a stronger-sounding answer. Media analysis must not be advertised until the released system proves actual byte processing by a capable service.

### Pillar 3 — A Better Next Conversation

Drivable should help users ask a mechanic, seller, buyer, or inspector better questions.

### Pillar 4 — Uncertainty You Can See

Possible causes, confidence, contradictions, and missing evidence are visible rather than buried.

### Pillar 5 — Human Judgment When It Matters

Higher-risk or materially uncertain cases may need human review or hands-on help.

## 7. Voice and Visual Direction

### Voice

- Calm, capable, plainspoken, and respectful.
- Never alarmist, smug, overly technical, or anti-mechanic.
- Short sentences around risk and next actions.
- Prefer “Here is what the evidence supports” over “Our AI knows.”
- Prefer “possible cause” over “diagnosis.”

### Visual Direction

Aim for modern automotive editorial photography rather than sci-fi AI imagery:

- Real, familiar vehicles and environments.
- Natural light, believable wear, ordinary clothing, and candid body language.
- Close details: dashboard, phone in a parked vehicle, repair paperwork, tire/wheel, seller photos, conversation at a shop.
- Human moments showing relief through clarity, not exaggerated celebration.
- Deep navy and charcoal foundation, cyan/teal signal color, restrained green for progress, amber for caution, and red only for genuine stop-level safety.
- Generous negative space for platform-native copy.

Avoid:

- Robots, holograms, glowing brains, floating code, impossible dashboards, luxury-only imagery, pristine generic supercars, oversized AI symbols, fake mechanic uniforms, extra fingers, illegible interface text, and dramatic roadside danger staged as entertainment.

Generated images should normally contain no embedded text. Typography and claims should be added in editable templates so wording can be reviewed and localized.

## 8. Launch Stages and Gates

### Stage 0 — Foundation (Now)

Build:

- Brand architecture and provisional visual system.
- Message house and claim rules.
- Complete asset manifest and file system.
- Waitlist/beta recruitment kit.
- Evergreen educational content.
- Product walkthrough and report-demo briefs using clearly labeled samples.
- Creative templates without performance claims.

Exit gate:

- Product naming decision.
- Trademark/domain review started.
- Safety and marketing claim review approved.
- Landing-page journey and analytics plan defined.

### Stage 1 — Closed Beta Recruitment

Build/use:

- Founder-led outreach.
- Local/community recruitment creative.
- Tester orientation and consent materials.
- Beta walkthrough video.
- Feedback and testimonial-permission workflow.

Do not use:

- Mass paid acquisition.
- “Proven,” “accurate,” “trusted by,” “saves,” or outcome claims.

Exit gate:

- Reliable intake-to-report delivery.
- Review and support service levels met.
- Critical safety cases handled correctly.
- Privacy, correction, and deletion paths work.
- Sufficient beta evidence for product and message decisions.

### Stage 2 — Proof Collection and Creative Validation

Build:

- Permissioned customer stories.
- Verified case studies with carefully scoped outcomes.
- Product demonstration footage.
- Organic creative testing across hooks and audiences.
- Landing-page A/B variants.
- Small capped paid tests if operational readiness permits.

Exit gate:

- Approved substantiation file for every performance claim.
- Conversion, support, refund/correction, and safety metrics inside thresholds.
- Several creative concepts perform across more than one audience segment.

### Stage 3 — Public Launch

Coordinate:

- Website launch and product availability.
- Founder story and press kit.
- Hero video and short-form cutdowns.
- Creator/community outreach.
- Search, social, retargeting, email, and organic publishing.
- Launch-day monitoring and incident response.

Budget expansion is gradual. Increase spend only when conversion quality, support capacity, and safety outcomes remain acceptable.

### Stage 4 — Scale and Optimization

- Expand winning audiences and creative families.
- Refresh creative before fatigue.
- Build separate Buyer Check, Seller Check/ClearSale, and Mechanic Match campaigns.
- Localize only after claims, safety language, and product delivery are ready for each region.

## 9. Channel Strategy

### Owned

- Product website and scenario landing pages.
- Email onboarding, education, report follow-up, and launch sequence.
- Blog/resource center centered on decisions and evidence, not generic repair instructions.
- Shareable sample reports and transparent methodology pages.

### Organic Social

- YouTube Shorts, Instagram Reels, TikTok: situation-first stories and evidence tips.
- YouTube long-form: founder demonstrations, real beta cases with permission, and mechanic conversations.
- Facebook/community groups: educational posts and controlled beta recruitment, respecting group rules.
- LinkedIn: founder/build story, safety design, and partnership narrative rather than consumer acquisition.

### Paid

- Search: high-intent queries around warning lights, repair decisions, used-car evaluation, and mechanic questions.
- Meta: relatable owner/buyer/seller scenarios with strong landing-page continuity.
- YouTube: problem-recognition and product-demonstration video.
- Retargeting: education and proof, never fabricated urgency.

Paid channels remain blocked until tracking, consent, landing pages, support, and budget controls are operational.

### Earned and Partnerships

- Automotive consumer reporters, local media, repair educators, used-car educators, community organizations, driving schools, roadside/towing partners, independent shops, and consumer-protection voices.
- Partnerships must preserve Drivable's uncertainty language and must not imply an endorsement that does not exist.

## 10. Content System

Use four repeatable series so the brand feels coherent:

1. **What changed?** — symptom/timing education.
2. **What would help?** — safe evidence collection.
3. **What is still unknown?** — confidence and missing evidence.
4. **What should I ask next?** — mechanic, seller, buyer, and inspector questions.

Every content item should do one job, use one primary CTA, and carry the minimum necessary boundary language.

## 11. Human-Looking Production Standard

“Not AI-looking” means truthful and well-produced, not concealing required disclosures.

- Prefer real product capture, founder footage, real vehicles, and permissioned beta stories for hero assets.
- Use generated imagery for concepting, backgrounds, scenario illustrations, and scalable secondary creative.
- Do not fabricate a customer, mechanic, testimonial, repair result, interface state, or press quote.
- Preserve natural asymmetry, plausible environments, restrained retouching, and real-world texture.
- Use professional typography and layout outside the image generator.
- Maintain editable masters, source provenance, model/prompt record for generated material, releases, and license records.
- Review every image at full size for hands, controls, dashboards, vehicle geometry, reflections, text, badges, license plates, and safety behavior.

## 12. Required Founder Participation

The founder does not need to become a marketer or developer. The high-value founder tasks are:

- Confirm final brand architecture and legal/company naming.
- Secure domains, trademarks/legal review, and platform accounts when recommended.
- Appear in founder video or approve a spokesperson alternative.
- Provide access to real vehicles and safe filming locations.
- Recruit and communicate with beta testers.
- Obtain signed media/testimonial permissions using approved forms.
- Approve budgets and payment methods; never paste secrets or full payment details into chat.
- Give final approval on factual company history and personal story.

Codex should provide exact checklists and copy/paste instructions when one of these actions becomes necessary.

## 13. Launch Readiness Stop Conditions

Pause advertising or publication if:

- A report or ad presents an unverified claim as fact.
- Product behavior implies safety clearance or confirmed diagnosis.
- Review/support queues exceed the promised service level.
- A privacy, security, adverse-event, or misleading-claim concern is unresolved.
- A landing page promises an unavailable feature.
- Tracking cannot distinguish beta/test traffic from real customers.
- Spend rises without confirmed conversion quality or operational capacity.
