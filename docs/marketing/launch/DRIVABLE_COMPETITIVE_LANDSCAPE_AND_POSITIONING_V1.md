# Drivable Competitive Landscape and Positioning V1

**Research date:** 2026-08-24  
**Scope:** Public first-party product pages and official store/support listings.  
**Purpose:** Inform product positioning and launch strategy; not a market-share study, legal opinion, or claim that every feature was independently tested.

## 1. Executive Finding

The automotive decision-support field is active and fragmented across five established product patterns:

1. OBD hardware and apps that decode trouble codes and suggest repairs.
2. Vehicle-history products that aggregate prior records.
3. Human pre-purchase inspection services.
4. Repair-price, shop-network, and booking marketplaces.
5. Remote expert-question services.

Drivable should not claim to be the first or only automotive AI, diagnostic aid, buyer tool, seller marketplace, mechanic network, or human-review service.

The strongest working position is a product-system promise:

> Drivable is designed to preserve one trustworthy evidence chain across an owner concern, Buyer Check, seller disclosure, human review, mechanic handoff, and later outcomes—without turning claims into facts or uncertainty into a diagnosis.

This remains an architectural position until the released product proves durable evidence storage, provenance, permissions, reuse, and handoff. Until then, say `designed around a connected evidence chain`, not `provides a complete evidence backbone`.

## 2. Landscape Map

| Category | Representative services | Publicly described strengths | Boundary relevant to Drivable |
|---|---|---|---|
| OBD diagnostics | FIXD, BlueDriver, Carly, Innova RepairSolutions2 | Code reading, repair suggestions, live data, maintenance, hardware-connected reports | Strong structured vehicle data; generally centered on compatible OBD hardware and supported vehicles |
| Vehicle history | CARFAX, carVertical | Prior-event and source-aggregated history data | Historical records complement but do not replace current condition inspection |
| Human pre-purchase inspection | Lemon Squad | On-location human visual inspection, photos, report, optional history add-on | Strong human observation; published limits acknowledge non-disassembly and hidden conditions |
| Repair marketplace/network | RepairPal, Openbay, YourMechanic | Estimates, certified/vetted networks, quotes, booking, warranties, mobile service | Strong provider supply and transaction fulfillment; Drivable has no equivalent live network today |
| Remote expert Q&A | JustAnswer | Connection to credentialed vehicle experts | Human question-answer model rather than persistent cross-journey vehicle evidence architecture |
| Vehicle rental/listing marketplace | drivable.app | Sports/luxury rental listings and AI-assisted listing creation | Creates an active naming and marketplace-position collision with Drivable |

## 3. OBD and Diagnostic Products

### FIXD

FIXD publicly describes a Bluetooth sensor and app that decode check-engine information, show issue severity, provide maintenance alerts, offer DIY materials, connect to a mechanic hotline, and locate repair shops. Its page uses strong claims about diagnosis, savings, fair pricing, and safe-to-drive guidance. Source: [FIXD official product site](https://www.fixd.com/).

**Implication:** `Plain-English vehicle answers`, `possible causes`, `mechanic help`, `repair cost`, and `shop finding` are not open positioning territory by themselves. Drivable must differentiate through evidence provenance, explicit unknowns, seller/buyer continuity, and bounded human handoff—not simply friendlier diagnostics.

### BlueDriver

BlueDriver publicly describes an OBD-II tool with code translation, vehicle-specific Repair Reports, Mode 6 results, freeze-frame data, smog readiness, and live sensor data. It says its repair guidance draws on millions of verified fixes. Source: [BlueDriver official site](https://us.bluedriver.com/).

**Implication:** Drivable should not imply that combining vehicle-specific history with likely fixes is novel. If Drivable uses OBD data, it must state exactly which codes/frames reach the system and how they influence the result.

### Carly

Carly's official App Store listing describes OBD diagnostics, live data, ECU access on supported vehicles, repair guides, maintenance, coding, and a used-car inspection feature. The listing also says feature availability varies by vehicle. Source: [Carly official App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/carly-obd2-car-scanner/id467344155).

**Implication:** Diagnosis plus DIY plus buyer-check language already coexist in one product. Drivable's defensible distinction must be demonstrated provenance and cross-workflow continuity, not merely having owner and buyer modes.

### Innova RepairSolutions2

Innova describes RepairSolutions2 as a hardware-linked service that saves diagnostic data, provides vehicle-specific fixes sourced from ASE-technician repair data, offers instructions and videos, includes service bulletins and recalls, and presents predicted repairs and maintenance. Sources: [RepairSolutions2 official overview](https://help.repairsolutions.com/article/2295-about-repairsolutions2) and [official feature/support index](https://help.repairsolutions.com/article/2253-repairsolutions2-support-features).

**Implication:** Drivable cannot use `verified fixes`, predictive repair, recall, or service-history language unless it has the corresponding sources, validation, and rights. Up-to-three possible causes should remain visibly different from a verified repair record.

## 4. Buyer Evidence and Inspection Products

### CARFAX

CARFAX describes its Vehicle History Report as one tool to use alongside a vehicle inspection and test drive. Source: [CARFAX company overview](https://www.carfax.com/company/about).

**Implication:** Buyer Check should position history data, submitted evidence, seller claims, and physical inspection as complementary sources. It must never imply that absence from a history source proves absence of damage or condition.

### carVertical

carVertical says it collects vehicle-history data from more than one thousand sources and uses statistical models to enrich information such as mileage trends. Its store listing describes possible mileage, damage, theft, prior-use, photo, ownership, service, and pricing information when available. Sources: [carVertical data-source explanation](https://www.carvertical.com/en/help/about-the-service/where-does-the-data-come-from) and [official Google Play listing](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.cvmobileapp).

**Implication:** Aggregated history and statistical enrichment are established. Drivable should disclose source availability by field and avoid a universal `verified` label.

### Lemon Squad

Lemon Squad offers on-location pre-purchase inspections and explicitly publishes meaningful limitations: visual inspection of accessible items, no complete disassembly, no guarantee, no title/VIN-authenticity determination, and possible differences between inspection time and purchase time. Sources: [Lemon Squad official service](https://lemonsquad.com/) and [inspection terms and limitations](https://lemonsquad.com/order/inspection_request).

**Implication:** Buyer Check should not compete by sounding more certain than a physical inspection service. It should make the difference between remote submitted evidence and a hands-on inspection unmistakable.

## 5. Mechanic Networks and Human Help

### RepairPal

RepairPal describes a location-specific Fair Price Estimator, certified-shop network, some instant pricing/appointments, and a price guarantee at participating shops. It explains that estimates use parts, labor-time, and local-rate inputs and are presented as ranges. Sources: [RepairPal FAQ](https://repairpal.com/faq) and [Certified network description](https://repairpal.com/certified).

**Implication:** Repair price ranges and certified-shop positioning require real data and program operations. Drivable should not market `fair price`, `certified`, or `vetted` until it builds and audits an equivalent defined process.

### Openbay

Openbay describes a workflow where users describe service or symptoms, receive local-shop quotes, compare pricing/reviews/location/availability, and book. It publishes provider vetting criteria including insurance, time in business, warranty, pricing, service, and technician certification. Sources: [Openbay marketplace](https://app.openbay.com/) and [official process and vetting criteria](https://www.openbay.com/how-openbay-works).

**Implication:** `Vetted provider matching` is a substantive operating claim, not a label. Mechanic Match currently remains request/referral support and must not borrow marketplace language.

### YourMechanic

YourMechanic describes quote and booking flows for mobile mechanics, at-home/office service, diagnostics, broad geographic coverage, online accounts, service history, and a service warranty. Sources: [YourMechanic official site](https://www.yourmechanic.com/) and [official support overview](https://www.yourmechanic.com/support).

**Implication:** Convenience, mobile service, instant quotes, booking, history, and warranty are established marketplace dimensions. Drivable should not imply those capabilities before fulfillment exists.

### JustAnswer

JustAnswer publicly offers access to vehicle experts. Its expert requirements page says vehicle experts need a recognized mechanic/technician license or certification plus relevant experience. Sources: [JustAnswer official site](https://www.justanswer.com/) and [official vehicle-expert requirements](https://experthelp.justanswer.com/hc/en-us/articles/4405473804941-Become-a-Vehicle-or-Boat-Expert-on-JustAnswer).

**Implication:** Human expert escalation is not unique. Drivable's potential advantage is a permissioned evidence package and provenance-preserving handoff, if implemented.

## 6. Naming-Collision Products

### drivable.app

The active `drivable.app` marketplace uses Drivable for sports/luxury vehicle rental and `Drivable AI` for assisted listing creation. Source: [official Drivable listing guide](https://www.drivable.app/en/help/hosts-managing-listings/create-a-listing-list-your-vehicle-on-drivable).

### ClearSale

The active ClearSale business at `clear.sale` describes ecommerce fraud prevention using AI and human analysis and identifies itself as part of Experian. Sources: [ClearSale official site](https://www.clear.sale/) and [official company page](https://www.clear.sale/about).

These are factual collision signals, not legal conclusions. Qualified counsel and the owner must decide coexistence, renaming, filing, geography, and accepted risk.

## 7. Positioning White Space

### Strongest direction

`One evidence chain. Every claim keeps its source.`

Expanded:

`Drivable is being designed so evidence collected for an owner concern can support the next authorized decision—Buyer Check, seller disclosure, human review, mechanic handoff, and later outcomes—while claims, observations, verification, conflicts, and unknowns remain distinct.`

### Why it is defensible

- It describes an architecture rather than claiming an empty category.
- It respects competitors' established strengths.
- It connects Drivable Check, Buyer Check, seller workflows, human review, and Mechanic Match coherently.
- It creates measurable product requirements: provenance, authorization, retention, correction, reuse, and outcome capture.
- It reinforces safe uncertainty rather than competing on maximal diagnostic confidence.

### Proof required before public use as current capability

- persistent vehicle/evidence identity;
- actual media and OBD byte ingestion where claimed;
- source/provenance labels at field level;
- user-authorized reuse across workflows;
- durable retention and deletion/export behavior;
- human-review record and scope;
- provider handoff with least-data sharing;
- outcome record linked without rewriting earlier claims;
- report versioning and correction history;
- privacy, terms, consent, and support aligned to behavior.

## 8. Messaging Hierarchy

1. **Brand idea:** Evidence before certainty.
2. **System value:** One connected evidence chain across vehicle decisions.
3. **Trust mechanism:** Claims, observations, verification, conflicts, and unknowns stay distinct.
4. **Primary products:** Drivable Check and Buyer Check.
5. **Human boundary:** Human or in-person review where safety or material uncertainty requires it.
6. **Expansion:** Seller disclosure, a renamed/cleared marketplace concept, Mechanic Match, and later Guided Service.
7. **Limitation:** Not a confirmed diagnosis, inspection, title determination, safety decision, repair guarantee, or transaction guarantee.

## 9. Claims Drivable Must Avoid

- first or only AI vehicle platform;
- first end-to-end vehicle evidence system;
- nobody else connects diagnosis, buyers, sellers, and mechanics;
- more accurate than OBD tools, inspectors, mechanics, or history reports;
- safest, most trustworthy, most transparent, or impossible to mislead;
- verified sellers, honest marketplace, vetted mechanics, or fair pricing without operational proof;
- analysis of photos, video, audio, or vibration without end-to-end modality proof;
- complete vehicle history, condition, title, inspection, diagnosis, or safety status;
- trademark-cleared, protected, or available for registration.

## 10. Strategic Recommendation

Do not attempt to outspend established category leaders on generic `car diagnosis`, `vehicle history`, `inspection`, or `find a mechanic` language at launch. Coordinate attention around a tangible product demonstration of provenance:

1. One synthetic vehicle record begins with an owner concern.
2. Every evidence item shows its source and processing state.
3. The report shows possible causes plus missing/conflicting evidence.
4. Buyer Check reuses only authorized evidence and preserves seller claims.
5. Human review adds a scoped observation without erasing model uncertainty.
6. A mechanic handoff contains only authorized relevant evidence.
7. A later outcome updates the record without retroactively making the original guess a fact.

That demonstration becomes the launch narrative only after the released product performs it end to end.
