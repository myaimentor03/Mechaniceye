# Drivable Creative Copy Template Library V1

These templates are production-ready starting points. Replace bracketed fields only with approved facts and links. Do not remove boundary language when it is necessary to prevent a misleading impression.

## 1. Core Brand Lines

### Primary

- A clearer next move for a confusing vehicle decision.
- Start with what happened. See what the evidence supports.
- Understand what is possible, what is missing, and what to ask next.

### Supporting

- Vehicle decision support for everyday owners, buyers, and sellers.
- Organize symptoms, evidence, possible causes, confidence, and next questions.
- Uncertainty should be explained—not hidden.

### Avoid as Primary Brand Lines

- Diagnose your car instantly.
- Know exactly what is wrong.
- Never get ripped off again.
- Save money on every repair.
- Drive with confidence.

## 2. Drivable Check Ad Templates

### Concept A — Something Changed

**Headline:** Something changed with the car. Start there.

**Body:** Tell Drivable what happened, when it happens, and what evidence you can gather safely. See possible causes, confidence, missing information, and useful next questions.

**CTA:** Start a Drivable Check

**Boundary:** Informational guidance based on information provided. Not a confirmed diagnosis, inspection, emergency service, or safety clearance.

### Concept B — Warning Light

**Headline:** A warning light is information—not the whole answer.

**Body:** Organize the exact light, symptoms, timing, recent work, and available OBD information before the next decision.

**CTA:** Organize what happened

**Safety line:** Red brake, oil-pressure, overheating, smoke, fire, fuel, steering, or severe drivability warnings may require stopping and qualified help.

### Concept C — Weird Noise

**Headline:** Hard to explain the noise? Explain when it happens.

**Body:** Timing, location, operating conditions, and a safely captured sound can help narrow the next questions—even when remote evidence cannot confirm the cause.

**CTA:** Build a clearer symptom story

### Concept D — Repair Estimate

**Headline:** Before approving parts, ask what the evidence supports.

**Body:** Compare what is known, what still needs checking, and what questions may help with the repair decision.

**CTA:** Review the next decision

**Boundary:** Drivable does not overrule hands-on inspection or guarantee repair cost, savings, or outcome.

## 6. Educational Carousel Templates

### Carousel A — What Evidence Helps?

1. **Cover:** A better vehicle question starts with better context.
2. **What changed?** Describe the symptom in your own words.
3. **When?** Note speed, RPM, temperature, braking, turning, starting, or weather conditions.
4. **What appeared?** Capture the exact warning light or message while safely parked.
5. **What happened before it?** Add recent repairs, maintenance, impacts, or long storage.
6. **What can be described safely?** Timing, dashboard messages, recent work, written observations, and user-entered OBD information may help. Unsupported media processing is not claimed.
7. **What should not be captured?** Do not drive and record, recreate severe symptoms, or reach near moving/hot parts.
8. **Close:** Start with what happened. See what the evidence supports.

### Carousel B — Possible Cause Is Not Diagnosis

1. **Cover:** Possible cause ≠ confirmed diagnosis.
2. A symptom can have more than one plausible explanation.
3. Good guidance shows the evidence supporting each path.
4. It also shows contradictions and what is missing.
5. Confidence describes the current inference—not a guarantee.
6. Hands-on inspection may still be the responsible next step.
7. **Close:** Uncertainty should be explained, not hidden.

### Carousel C — Seller Claim Versus Observation

1. **Cover:** “Seller says” and “observed” are not the same.
2. **Seller claim:** “The brakes were just replaced.”
3. **Supporting document:** A receipt reports brake work on a stated date.
4. **Observation:** A qualified inspection describes the current brake condition.
5. A receipt does not prove current condition or repair success.
6. Missing contradiction is not verification.
7. **Close:** Buyer Check keeps the sources visible.

## 7. Six-Second Video Hooks

- Something changed with the car. What should happen next?
- A warning light is not the whole story.
- Before approving parts, ask what the evidence supports.
- Before you chase the listing, check what is missing.
- “Seller says” is not the same as “observed.”
- You do not need car terminology. Start with what happened.

## 8. Fifteen-Second Video Structure

**0–3 seconds:** Recognizable situation and one hook.  
**3–9 seconds:** Show the evidence/problem being organized.  
**9–12 seconds:** Show one useful output: missing information, confidence, or questions.  
**12–15 seconds:** CTA and concise boundary.

Example:

> “A warning light came on, but that is not the whole story. Drivable organizes what happened, when it happened, available codes, and what is still missing. Start a Drivable Check. Not a diagnosis or safety clearance.”

## 9. Caption Closers

- Based on the information provided; hands-on inspection may still be needed.
- Do not take a safety risk to collect evidence.
- A possible cause is not a confirmed diagnosis.
- Buyer Check is not a pre-purchase inspection or title verification.
- Seller statements remain seller-reported unless independently verified.
- Mechanic Match does not guarantee availability, pricing, quality, or outcome.

## 10. CTA Hierarchy

### Awareness

- See how Drivable works.
- Learn what evidence helps.
- See how confidence works.

### Consideration

- View a sample report.
- Review the listing.
- Compare the next paths.

### Conversion

- Start a Drivable Check.
- Apply to test Drivable.
- Start Buyer Check.
- Prepare a Seller Check.
- Build my mechanic summary.

Use one primary CTA per asset. Do not pair “urgent” creative with a slow beta/application CTA when immediate qualified help may be required.
