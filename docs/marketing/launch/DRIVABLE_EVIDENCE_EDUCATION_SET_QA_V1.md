## Complete organic captions

### Card 1

Start with what changed, not a guessed part. Write down when the symptom occurred, what you could safely notice, what changed recently, and what remains unknown.

Park first. Never record, type, photograph, or connect equipment while driving. Do not recreate a potentially dangerous symptom for content.

Educational information only. Not a diagnosis or vehicle-safety determination.

### Card 2

Timing and conditions can make a vehicle note more useful. Was the vehicle cold or warm? Starting, braking, turning, or idling? Had weather, fuel, service, load, or driving conditions changed?

Write from memory after parking. “I do not know” is better than guessing, and no one should take a risk to collect evidence.

Educational information only. Not a diagnosis, inspection, or emergency service.

### Card 3

If you already have an OBD code, record the exact letters and numbers, when it was read, whether the warning light was active, and which device or source displayed it.

A code can report a detected condition without proving which part failed. Testing and inspection may still be necessary. The P0301 example shown is synthetic education—not a result from a real vehicle.

Educational information only. Not a diagnosis or parts recommendation.

### Card 4

Missing evidence belongs in the record. “Not available,” “not sure,” and “not observed” are different from “no problem.” They mean the evidence does not answer the question yet.

Drivable is designed to preserve those limits instead of filling the gap with invented certainty.

Educational information only. Missing evidence is not a vehicle-safety clearance.

## Alt text

- **Card 1:** Dark Drivable educational card titled “Observe. Don't guess.” It lists when a symptom happens, what can be safely noticed, what changed recently, and what remains unknown, followed by “Park first. Evidence second.”
- **Card 2:** Light Drivable card titled “Capture the conditions. Not a guessed cause.” It organizes useful written details into when, action, and context and includes a stronger example note plus a warning not to recreate a symptom.
- **Card 3:** Dark Drivable card reading “A code reports a condition. Not always the culprit.” It uses a clearly synthetic P0301 example and lists the exact code, time/light state, and device source as useful context.
- **Card 4:** Light Drivable card titled “Missing evidence belongs in the record.” It distinguishes not available, not sure, and not observed and explains that missing does not mean no problem.

## Capability and safety review

- The set describes written observations, timing, recent context, dashboard information, user-entered OBD information, and missing evidence only.
- It does not claim that photo, audio, video, document, computer-vision, sound, or vibration bytes reach or influence a capable service.
- “Vibration” on card 1 is a person's safely remembered observation, not sensor capture or vibration analysis.
- The OBD example is visibly and textually identified as synthetic education.
- No code is treated as a failed-part diagnosis or repair instruction.
- The set repeatedly instructs the user to park first and never recreate a symptom or handle equipment while driving.
- None of the cards declares a vehicle safe or says missing evidence means no issue exists.
