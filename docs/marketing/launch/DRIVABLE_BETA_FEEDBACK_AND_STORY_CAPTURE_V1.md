# Drivable Beta Feedback and Story Capture V1

**Purpose:** Collect product feedback, outcomes, and potential stories as three separate activities with separate permissions.  
**Status:** Operational template; privacy and legal review are required before collecting identifiable media or granting public-use rights.

## 1. Separation Rule

Participation in the beta must not require a testimonial, public story, marketing release, public identity, or permission to use identifiable evidence.

Keep these separate:

1. **Service feedback** — Was the experience understandable, useful, and safe?
2. **Outcome follow-up** — What action occurred and what was later observed or verified?
3. **Marketing story permission** — May Drivable use a specific approved story, quote, image, video, or evidence excerpt publicly?

Declining the third must not affect service access, support, or treatment.

## 2. Immediate Experience Interview

Ask shortly after the user reviews the beta experience.

### Understanding

- In your own words, what did Drivable tell you?
- What did it explicitly not tell you?
- Did you understand the difference between a possible cause and a confirmed diagnosis?
- Did you understand the safety/inspection limitations?
- What felt most certain? Was that level of certainty appropriate?

### Usefulness

- What decision were you trying to make?
- Which part helped most: safety routing, evidence organization, possible causes, confidence, missing information, questions, or mechanic/buyer handoff?
- What did you expect to receive that was not present?
- What would you do next based on the information?

### Friction

- Which question or instruction was confusing?
- Was any requested evidence difficult or unsafe to obtain?
- Did the process ask for information you did not want to provide?
- Did any wording sound judgmental, alarming, or too confident?
- Where did you consider abandoning the process?

### Trust

- What made the experience feel credible or not credible?
- Were claims, observations, and unknowns clearly separated?
- Would you feel comfortable sharing the handoff with a mechanic or inspector? Why?
- Did anything imply a guarantee, inspection, or safety clearance?

## 3. Buyer Check Interview

- Which seller claims mattered most to your decision?
- Could you tell which details were seller-reported versus observed or document-supported?
- Which unknown or contradiction changed your next step?
- Did Buyer Check help you prepare an inspection request or seller question?
- Did anything make the vehicle seem approved, safe, or verified when it was not?
- Did you inspect, negotiate, pause, or walk away? Why?
- What information remained unavailable?

## 4. Outcome Follow-Up

Ask only after enough time has passed for an action or finding.

### Outcome Fields

- Action taken: no action, monitored, sought inspection, repaired, sought second opinion, bought, did not buy, sold, did not sell, or other.
- Who reported the outcome.
- Date and vehicle mileage.
- Inspection or repair provider, if the participant chooses to identify them.
- Finding or work performed in the source's own wording.
- Supporting document/media availability and permission scope.
- Whether the original possible causes included the later finding.
- Whether confidence and urgency felt appropriate in retrospect.
- Cost information, exact source, inclusions/exclusions, and whether public use is permitted.
- What remains unknown.

### Verification Labels

- `participant_reported`
- `document_supported`
- `professional_observed`
- `independently_verified`
- `contradicted`
- `unknown`

Never relabel a participant report as verified merely because it is plausible or favorable.

## 5. Potential Story Interview

Conduct only after the participant separately expresses interest in sharing.

- What was happening before you used Drivable?
- What were you trying to decide—not what result did we want to advertise?
- What evidence did you have at the time?
- What did Drivable show as possible, supported, missing, or uncertain?
- What question or next action did it help you prepare?
- What happened afterward, and how do you know?
- What would you tell someone not to assume from your experience?
- Which details must remain private?
- Are you comfortable using your name, first name only, initials, pseudonym, or no identity?
- Which images, audio, video, screenshots, documents, or vehicle details may be used?
- Where may the story appear, and for how long?

## 6. Story Candidate Evaluation

Use a story only if:

- The participant freely opted in.
- The service and outcome records are linked but permission scopes remain separate.
- Material facts are supported at the level claimed.
- Uncertainty, contradictory evidence, and limitations are not edited away.
- The story does not encourage unsafe action.
- The participant is not presented as a typical outcome without evidence supporting typicality.
- The story does not accuse a seller, mechanic, shop, or third party without appropriate substantiation and review.
- Identifiable vehicle, location, account, title, receipt, VIN, and contact information is minimized.
- The exact final copy and media receive participant approval when required by policy/release.

Reject a story even with permission when it would be misleading or unsafe.

## 7. Quote Editing Rules

Allowed:

- Correct obvious spelling or remove filler without changing meaning.
- Shorten a quote while preserving context.
- Add bracketed context sparingly and transparently.
- Paraphrase only when labeled as a summary rather than quotation.

Not allowed:

- Combine statements from different moments into a stronger result.
- Change “helped me ask” into “diagnosed.”
- Change “I decided not to buy” into “Drivable saved me thousands.”
- Remove a participant's uncertainty or inspection dependency.
- Present an incentivized quote without required disclosure.

## 8. Story Artifact Package

Every public story needs:

- Story ID and internal owner.
- Participant/release reference stored in a restricted system.
- Product and case type.
- Timeline of intake, report, action, and outcome.
- Evidence and verification table.
- Exact approved quote and edited variants.
- Claims substantiation record.
- Approved identity level and media list.
- Required disclosures and limitations.
- Channels, geography, duration, and paid-use status.
- Expiry/review date.
- Final creative, copy, destination, and publication log.

## 9. Incentives

- Beta compensation or service access must not require a positive review.
- Any story-specific compensation is disclosed and documented.
- Never buy star ratings, positive sentiment, or silence about a negative experience.
- Do not condition support, correction, refund, or privacy rights on removing criticism.

## 10. Interviewer Script

> Thank you for helping us test Drivable. First, we want honest product feedback; positive or negative answers are equally useful. This conversation does not require you to provide a testimonial or let us use your identity or vehicle information publicly. If we later think your experience could help explain the product, we will ask separately and show you the proposed scope before using it.

## 11. Stop and Escalate

Stop story work and route to the appropriate owner when the participant reports:

- Injury, crash, fire, unsafe driving, serious property damage, or another adverse event.
- A claim that Drivable caused or encouraged harm.
- A privacy/security concern or unintended disclosure.
- A dispute involving a seller, mechanic, provider, payment, title, or legal issue.
- A request to withdraw permission, correct facts, or remove public material.
- A vulnerable participant or circumstance requiring special review.

Do not continue a marketing interview while an adverse event or rights request is unresolved.
