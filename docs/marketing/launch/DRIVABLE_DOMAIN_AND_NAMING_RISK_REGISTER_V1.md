## 3. Naming Collision — Drivable

There is an active automotive service at [`drivable.app`](https://www.drivable.app/) using **Drivable** for a sports/luxury vehicle-rental marketplace. Its public help content also calls an automated listing feature **Drivable AI**, including AI-assisted registration-document scanning and vehicle-description extraction: [`Create a listing – List your vehicle on Drivable`](https://www.drivable.app/en/help/hosts-managing-listings/create-a-listing-list-your-vehicle-on-drivable).

Why this matters:

- identical consumer-facing word in the automotive category;
- overlapping vehicle marketplace/listing and AI language;
- plausible customer, search, social-handle, app-store, press, and partnership confusion;
- risk increases if Drivable markets ClearSale as a vehicle marketplace or uses `Drivable AI`.

Required action:

- qualified trademark counsel searches relevant jurisdictions, classes, registrations, applications, common-law use, domains, app stores, social handles, and marketplace context;
- document the intended legal owner, first-use evidence, geographic scope, product/service descriptions, and risk decision;
- do not use `Drivable AI` as a product or feature name;
- do not claim `first`, `only`, `original`, or category exclusivity;
- keep major public/paid brand assets reversible until the decision is recorded.

This record does not conclude whether the names can coexist.

## 4. Naming Collision — ClearSale

[`clear.sale`](https://www.clear.sale/) is an established ecommerce fraud-prevention service branded **ClearSale** and identifies itself as part of Experian. Its public materials describe AI plus human fraud analysis and broad commercial use. The company also publishes an [`About ClearSale`](https://www.clear.sale/about) page documenting its long-running fraud-prevention business and Experian relationship.

Why this matters:

- identical **ClearSale** word mark;
- both products concern transaction trust, claims, fraud/misrepresentation risk, and human-plus-AI review;
- plausible confusion in search, press, partnerships, software categories, and future marketplace or payment activity;
- acquiring `clear.sale` is not available as a simple naming solution because it is actively used.

Required action:

- treat **ClearSale** as a provisional internal product name pending qualified review;
- investigate alternatives without prematurely replacing the product architecture;
- do not buy major media, file trademark applications, commission permanent signage, print packaging, or build a public campaign around ClearSale without owner/legal direction;
- do not imply affiliation with Experian or the existing ClearSale business;
- keep generic descriptive copy such as `evidence-forward vehicle listings` separate from the provisional name.

This record does not conclude whether the names can coexist.

## 5. Defensible Positioning

Do not position Drivable as the first, only, or sole AI vehicle-support product. The working differentiator is:

> One trustworthy evidence chain across owner concerns, Buyer Check, seller disclosure, human/mechanic handoff, and outcomes—with claims, observations, verification, conflicts, and unknowns kept distinct.

This is a product-architecture position, not a proven superiority claim. Marketing must still verify that released workflows genuinely preserve the chain before presenting it as current behavior.

Approved supporting ideas:

- `Evidence collected once can support the next authorized vehicle decision.`
- `The source of a claim should remain visible from intake through handoff.`
- `Confidence should show support, conflict, and missing evidence.`
- `Human review belongs where safety or material uncertainty requires it.`

Restricted:

- `The only end-to-end vehicle evidence platform`
- `Nobody else connects the whole journey`
- `The first AI vehicle trust platform`
- `A category of one`
- `The only honest vehicle marketplace`

## 6. Reversibility Rules Until Clearance

- Continue strategy, evidence architecture, safety language, unbranded research, editable source templates, and provisional review media.
- Keep wordmarks, app icons, profile handles, motion, sonic mark, permanent URLs, app-store metadata, sponsorships, paid campaigns, creator whitelisting, and press launch assets in review or blocked state.
- Do not convert editable text to irreversible artwork at scale.
- Avoid embedding provisional product names in filmed dialogue, licensed music, physical merchandise, event displays, vehicle wraps, or long-lived paid inventory.
- Preserve source files so a rename can propagate systematically.
- Include a naming-decision checkpoint in every vendor statement of work.
