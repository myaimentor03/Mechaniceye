# Drivable Founder Build Journal V1

Ten repeatable founder-post formats for transparent pre-beta communication. These are not fictional diary entries; every post must be completed with something the founder or team actually did, learned, decided, or changed.

## Operating Rule

The build journal should make the work legible without manufacturing progress. Never claim a test happened, a customer benefited, a mechanic endorsed the product, a launch date exists, or a feature works unless the record supports it.

Use real founder/team photographs or workspace/product details only with permission and privacy review. Do not use generated portraits or staged “customer” images as documentary evidence.

## Post 1 — Why Drivable Exists

**Prompt:** What recurring vehicle decision felt unnecessarily confusing, and what harm did that confusion create?

**Structure:**

1. A specific founder-observed problem, with private details removed.
2. What existing process made difficult.
3. The principle Drivable will follow.
4. The current product stage.

**Close:** `We are building Drivable around evidence before certainty. Drivable Check and Buyer Check are preparing for closed beta.`

## Post 2 — A Promise We Refused to Make

**Prompt:** Which exciting marketing promise would be misleading today?

**Completed subject:** Why Drivable ranks possible causes instead of promising an exact diagnosis.

It would be easy to market Drivable as providing an exact diagnosis. We are not doing that because remote evidence can be incomplete, unclear, conflicting, or unable to reveal a condition that requires hands-on inspection. Drivable is designed to rank up to three possible causes when the evidence supports them, show missing information, and recommend human or in-person review when required. The distinction matters because confident wording must not encourage an unsafe decision or an unnecessary repair.

## Post 3 — What “Unknown” Means

**Prompt:** Show how missing evidence changes a result without exposing private submissions.

**Structure:** synthetic scenario → missing evidence → language that must remain uncertain → next useful evidence or human review.

Label the scenario synthetic. Do not imply model accuracy from one example.

## Post 4 — The Seller Claim Rule

**Prompt:** Explain why repetition or AI rewriting is not verification.

**Core line:** `A seller statement remains a seller statement until an appropriate verification method supports the exact field.`

Use the ClearSale provenance visual. State that ClearSale is coming soon.

## Post 5 — Respecting Good Mechanics

**Prompt:** What information can make the shop conversation more useful without pretending remote software replaces hands-on work?

Share estimate and evidence questions. Do not portray a common phrase, price difference, or recommendation as proof of dishonesty.

## Post 6 — A Beta Decision

**Prompt:** What did the team change after a real test or review?

Do not draft this post in advance. Publish it only after a real test record states the activity, factual observation, unproven questions, resulting decision, and next evidence. Until that record exists, this post is blocked rather than partially written.

Do not turn an internal usability observation into a customer-outcome claim.

## Post 7 — Safety by Design

**Prompt:** Show one safety behavior built into instructions, capture, review, or escalation.

Potential topics:

- park-first evidence collection;
- allowing `I can't provide this`;
- stopping a workflow for urgent warning signs;
- preventing a confidence score from becoming a safety rating;
- human review for safety or material uncertainty.

Do not reveal exploitable safety logic or imply the safeguards are exhaustive.

## Post 8 — What the Repository Can Do Today

**Prompt:** Give an honest readiness update.

Use the current release matrix. Distinguish:

- working controlled workflow;
- mock or demo behavior;
- manual pilot;
- feature in preparation;
- future roadmap.

Avoid progress percentages unless a defined denominator and method exist.

## Post 9 — What We Need From Beta Testers

**Prompt:** Explain the type of feedback needed and why praise is not the goal.

Ask about instruction clarity, safe evidence collection, confusing states, uncertainty, report usefulness, accessibility, privacy comfort, and support experience.

State that private feedback is not marketing permission.

## Post 10 — A Launch Gate

**Prompt:** Explain one condition Drivable will require before scaling.

Examples:

- a working support and incident process;
- verified analytics and consent;
- representative beta feedback;
- documented claim substantiation;
- stable product and demo flows;
- account and payment ownership;
- permissioned customer proof.

**Close:** `A massive launch should amplify something ready—not hide what is missing.`

## Reusable Post Footer

Choose only the relevant lines:

- `Drivable Check and Buyer Check are preparing for limited beta testing.`
- `Possible causes are not a confirmed diagnosis or safety determination.`
- `Never collect vehicle evidence while driving.`
- `ClearSale is coming soon.`
- `Mechanic Match is a manual referral pilot planned for a later stage.`
- `https://www.getdrivable.com/`

## Visual Frames

### Founder portrait

Natural light, ordinary working environment, direct but relaxed expression, no costume-like mechanic styling, no unsafe vehicle pose, no visible confidential material.

### Hands and work

Notebook, safely parked vehicle, paper flow, interface sketch, accessibility review, or product discussion. Avoid fake code screens, unreadable AI text, and confidential data.

### Product detail

Use synthetic demo data with a visible `Demo` label. Show one decision or evidence principle rather than a crowded dashboard.

### Text card

Use the Drivable editorial system: Midnight/Paper background, one short statement, a cyan evidence cue, and a visible stage/boundary label.

## Review Checklist

- [ ] The founder actually experienced or approved the described event.
- [ ] The post distinguishes observation, inference, decision, and aspiration.
- [ ] Current capability and roadmap are not blended.
- [ ] No private person, submission, vehicle, location, company, or document is identifiable without permission.
- [ ] Metrics and outcomes have a substantiation record.
- [ ] Quotes are exact or clearly approved edits.
- [ ] Product limitations appear when material.
- [ ] Image and music rights are documented.
- [ ] Comments have an assigned response owner.
- [ ] A correction can be issued without deleting the underlying record.
