# Drivable Sonic and Voice Direction V1

This document defines the working audio identity, voiceover direction, recording standards, licensing controls, and accessibility requirements for Drivable. It does not select a performer, generate a synthetic clone, license music, or authorize publication.

## 1. Audio Brand Principle

**Clarity arriving—not certainty being announced.**

The sound of Drivable should feel calm, attentive, human, and technically competent. It should reduce cognitive pressure around a vehicle decision without trivializing safety or imitating emergency, dealership, racing, or futuristic-AI advertising.

## 2. Sonic Traits

### Use

- restrained, warm, modern instrumentation;
- clear attack and short, natural decay;
- subtle acoustic or tactile textures;
- modest tempo and controlled dynamics;
- a small resolving interval that suggests organization rather than triumph;
- enough spectral space for speech intelligibility;
- quiet mechanical textures only when recorded safely and licensed.

### Avoid

- engine revs, tire squeals, horns, alarms, sirens, collision impacts, or warning chimes as decoration;
- suspense drones that sensationalize a vehicle concern;
- casino/game reward sounds for confidence or safety states;
- luxury-dealership gloss, hard-rock repair-shop clichés, or aggressive racing energy;
- robotic voices, sci-fi sweeps, digital “AI magic,” or synthetic diagnostic beeps;
- a triumphant resolution that implies a confirmed repair, safety clearance, or successful transaction;
- music that masks speech or depends on a platform's unverified commercial-use license.

## 3. Working Sonic Mark Brief — AU-001

**Length:** 1.0–1.8 seconds for the core sting; optional 3-second expanded version  
**Shape:** three quiet evidence points followed by one restrained resolving tone  
**Tempo feel:** approximately 84–96 BPM equivalent, without an obvious beat  
**Timbre:** soft mallet, muted pluck, felted key, or tactile electronic-acoustic hybrid  
**Pitch behavior:** small ascending motion, then a stable but non-triumphant resolution  
**Dynamic target:** clear at mobile volume without startling the listener  
**Variants:** full mix, speech-safe, UI-soft, one-color/acoustic equivalent, and silent visual-only companion

The three evidence points may echo the three-point brand symbol, but the sound must not be interpreted as three diagnostic answers or a completion/safety indicator.

## 4. UI Sound Boundary

Use no sound by default for safety, risk, confidence, or report conclusions. If interface sounds are later introduced:

- provide mute and reduced-sensory controls;
- never communicate information only through sound;
- distinguish submission, processing, success, warning, and failure without suggesting vehicle condition;
- do not play a celebratory sound when a possible cause ranks highly;
- do not imitate vehicle warning tones;
- test with screen readers, phone silent modes, hearing devices, and common mobile speakers.

## 5. Voiceover Character — AU-003

The recommended voice is:

- conversational, grounded, and respectful;
- steady without sounding clinical or detached;
- knowledgeable without impersonating a mechanic, engineer, doctor, inspector, or government authority;
- regionally understandable without erasing the performer's natural identity;
- able to speak safety boundaries plainly, without rushing or lowering volume;
- comfortable saying `possible`, `unknown`, and `we need more evidence` without apologizing.

Do not direct the performer to sound like:

- an emergency dispatcher;
- a stereotyped mechanic or salesperson;
- a documentary authority declaring facts;
- a cheerful assistant minimizing risk;
- a synthetic robot or well-known person;
- a frightened driver or angry customer.

## 6. Read Direction

- Target 135–155 spoken words per minute for education; slower for safety instructions.
- Pause before and after material limitations.
- Give `possible causes`, `missing evidence`, and `human review` equal semantic weight.
- Do not throw away disclaimers at the end or speed-read them.
- Pronounce **Drivable** naturally as `DRY-vuh-buhl` unless founder brand review chooses otherwise.
- Pronounce **Mechanic's Eye** as ordinary possessive English, not `mechanics eye`.
- Read URLs only when necessary and provide them in text/captions.

## 7. Sample Audition Copy

### Brand — 20 seconds

Something changed with the car. Before guessing at the answer, start with what happened, when it happens, and what evidence you can collect safely. Drivable helps organize possible causes, confidence, and what is still unknown—so the next question can be clearer. Evidence before certainty.

### Safety — 15 seconds

Park first. Evidence second. Never record, photograph, type, or connect equipment while driving. If a condition may be urgent or unsafe, stop using the vehicle and contact a qualified professional.

### Confidence — 15 seconds

Sometimes the available evidence is not enough to rank a possibility responsibly. Drivable should say that clearly—not turn a weak signal into a confident-sounding answer.

### Buyer Check — 15 seconds

A seller statement does not become a fact because AI rewrites it. Buyer Check is designed to keep claims, observations, verification, conflicts, and unknowns visibly distinct.

## 8. Human and Synthetic Voice Policy

### Human performer

Obtain a signed agreement covering recording scope, edits, channels, paid advertising, geography, term, exclusivity, reuse, derivative cutdowns, compensation, credits, archival use, termination, and prohibited voice cloning.

### Synthetic voice

Do not clone the founder, employee, actor, mechanic, customer, or any identifiable person without specific informed permission and a reviewed contract. Do not use a voice designed to imitate a public figure. Disclose synthetic voice where required or where omission could mislead.

Record the provider, model/voice identifier, license, commercial rights, generation date, script, settings, edits, reviewer, and permitted uses. Confirm that the provider's terms cover paid ads and do not create unacceptable training, retention, or identity risks.

### Preferred launch posture

Use a real, contracted human voice for founder and trust-sensitive brand stories. Synthetic voice may be evaluated for internal prototypes or clearly governed utility narration, not used merely to make a fabricated testimonial sound real.

## 9. Music and Sound Licensing

Before any track enters production, record:

- track title, composer, performer, publisher, label, and source;
- license type, receipt/agreement, account owner, purchase date, and cost;
- permitted platforms, paid advertising, broadcast, events, app use, podcasts, derivatives, geography, and term;
- attribution and cue-sheet requirements;
- content-ID/claim handling;
- restrictions on editing, stems, sublicensing, client work, and transfer;
- expiration, renewal, removal, and archive requirements.

Do not assume that music available inside a social platform is licensed for off-platform use, paid advertising, boosted posts, app distribution, or permanent brand assets.

## 10. Recording Standards

- Record lossless WAV at 48 kHz / 24-bit where practical.
- Capture clean room tone and alternate takes.
- Avoid destructive noise reduction, heavy compression, or artificial presence that sounds promotional.
- Maintain a raw, access-controlled source and a documented edit master.
- Mix narration for intelligibility across phone, laptop, vehicle cabin while parked, headphones, and assistive listening conditions.
- Deliver full mix, dialogue-only, music-only, effects-only, captions, transcript, and loudness record where applicable.
- Check current channel loudness and file requirements before export.

## 11. Accessibility

- Every spoken asset requires accurate captions and a transcript.
- Meaning conveyed by a sound must also be visible or spoken.
- Do not use sound to create urgency where no urgent condition has been established.
- Provide audio description when important visual evidence is not described by narration.
- Avoid rapid, startling, or repetitive effects.
- Respect reduced-motion and muted-autoplay environments with a complete silent experience.

- [ ] Safety and uncertainty language is spoken clearly.
- [ ] No voice or sound implies diagnosis, safety clearance, guaranteed outcome, or verified seller/provider status.
- [ ] Performer, music, effects, recording, and location rights are documented.
- [ ] Synthetic-media provenance and disclosure are recorded where applicable.
- [ ] Captions and transcript match the final edit.
- [ ] Names, technical terms, and URLs are pronounced correctly.
- [ ] Mix is intelligible on small speakers and does not startle.
