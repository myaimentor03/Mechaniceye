# Drivable 90-Day Beta-to-Launch Calendar V1

**Status:** Relative calendar; Day 1 begins only after the beta intake and operations gate are ready.  
**Purpose:** Coordinate product truth, beta evidence, creative production, operations, and launch without manufacturing an arbitrary public date.

## 1. Do Not Start the Clock Until

- Drivable Check and Buyer Check beta labels and scope are confirmed.
- The beta application, safety screen, consent, intake, notification, response, correction, and support paths work end to end.
- A named person owns review/support each day the beta accepts cases.
- Test data cannot be confused with customer-ready reports.
- The application flow has capacity limits and a pause control.
- Privacy, evidence handling, and deletion/correction responsibilities are assigned.
- The beta recruitment landing page matches actual availability.

If these are not true, remain in pre-beta preparation. Do not announce a countdown.

## 2. Days 1–14 — Controlled Beta Start

### Product and Operations

- Admit a very small number of appropriate non-emergency testers.
- Walk every case manually through intake, evidence, analysis, review, delivery, support, and follow-up.
- Record safety escalations, missing evidence, turnaround, reviewer effort, misunderstandings, corrections, and failures.
- Pause intake whenever the team cannot maintain the promised service.

### Marketing

- Use personal founder outreach and permission-based community recruitment.
- Publish only closed-beta and educational content.
- Use the “Something changed with the car. Start there.” recruitment concept.
- Do not run paid advertising.

### Creative Production

- Finalize brand lockup after rendered review and trademark screening.
- Capture founder interview and safe real-world b-roll if the beta UI is stable enough.
- Capture approved product screens using demo data.

### Gate at Day 14

Continue only if participants understand the limitations, operations are manageable, and no unresolved safety/privacy incident exists.

## 3. Days 15–30 — Message and Workflow Validation

### Product and Operations

- Expand gradually while preserving manual review.
- Compare Drivable Check versus Buyer Check demand, completion, and support burden.
- Identify evidence prompts that improve usefulness without unsafe collection.
- Measure where users believe the product promises more than it does.

### Marketing

- Test the four educational series organically:
  - What changed?
  - What evidence helps?
  - What is still unknown?
  - What should I ask next?
- Test message comprehension, not just engagement.
- Begin a founder build journal with factual beta-stage language.

### Creative Production

- Edit the founder-story master and beta walkthrough.
- Produce the six Drivable Check and four Buyer Check short-video pilots.
- Build square, portrait, story, and link-preview derivatives from approved masters.

### Gate at Day 30

Require stable intake/delivery, acceptable queue age, usable participant feedback, and no repeated dangerous misunderstanding.

## 4. Days 31–45 — Proof Collection

### Product and Operations

- Request optional outcomes separately from service feedback.
- Classify outcomes by source and verification level.
- Identify cases suitable for a story only after completion and review.
- Keep testimonial/media permission separate and optional.

### Marketing

- Draft case studies from permissioned facts without publishing them yet.
- Validate which claims can be substantiated and which remain blocked.
- Prepare press FAQ and founder facts.
- Begin partner conversations with a narrow feedback request, not an endorsement assumption.

### Creative Production

- Capture follow-up footage only for participants who signed the appropriate release.
- Edit one evidence-centered case story without implying general accuracy or savings.
- Refine product demonstration based on actual beta questions.

### Gate at Day 45

Do not advance proof claims unless source, scope, date, permission, limitations, and approved wording are documented.

## 5. Days 46–60 — Organic Launch Rehearsal

### Product and Operations

- Run a controlled traffic spike simulation.
- Test queue limits, pause controls, notifications, incident response, corrections, and monitoring.
- Freeze or explicitly version customer-facing behavior needed for launch creative.

### Marketing

- Publish a small coordinated organic campaign.
- Measure qualified conversion and expectation quality by concept.
- Test landing-page continuity and application quality.
- Build the public FAQ from real recurring questions.

### Creative Production

- Complete public-launch visual system and manually reframe all channel exports.
- Prepare hero film assembly; keep outcome-dependent scenes conditional.
- Complete accessibility, captions, alt text, rights, and provenance records.

### Gate at Day 60

Public-launch planning continues only if product reliability, support, safety, privacy, and message comprehension remain within approved thresholds under increased traffic.

## 6. Days 61–75 — Small Paid Validation and Press Preparation

This phase is optional. Skip paid media if organic demand already fills safe capacity.

### Paid Test

- Use a small capped budget and a single approved objective.
- Begin with Drivable Check or Buyer Check—never all products simultaneously.
- Test two or three validated concepts, not dozens of weak variations.
- Monitor qualified applications, support/review capacity, safety ineligibility, and misunderstanding daily.
- Pause according to the stop-loss runbook.

### Press and Partnerships

- Verify founder biography, company facts, availability, pricing, capacity, and contact details.
- Prepare press assets without sending them.
- Offer factual demos to a small relevant group; do not seek premature mass coverage.

### Gate at Day 75

Require acceptable qualified-acquisition cost, operating capacity, product health, and no unresolved claim/privacy/safety issue before public scale.

## 7. Days 76–89 — Launch Readiness Freeze

- Set the launch scope, geography, capacity, availability, price, and exact claims.
- Finalize the launch page, product walkthrough, hero/cutdowns, screenshots, email, press kit, and support materials.
- Complete end-to-end test on real target devices and browsers.
- Re-verify every link, form, event, notification, disclosure, release, and account owner.
- Rehearse launch-day command roles and pause/takedown procedures.
- Schedule but do not blindly auto-publish high-risk messages.
- Establish backup assets and a no-proof version of every campaign.

No material product, price, claim, or UI change after creative freeze without impact review and re-approval.

## 8. Day 90 — Public Launch Window

Day 90 is a readiness window, not a promise. Launch only if the go/no-go review passes.

### Launch Sequence

1. Verify product, support, review, analytics, and incident channels.
2. Publish owned website and availability statement.
3. Send the permissioned email announcement.
4. Publish founder/product organic media.
5. Begin press/partner outreach.
6. Activate paid media gradually, if approved.
7. Monitor product health, queue, support, safety, conversion quality, and spend.

### Do Not

- Release every campaign simultaneously without monitoring.
- Expand spend during an unresolved incident.
- hide limited capacity or changing beta/public availability.
- use proof claims that missed final substantiation.

## 9. Days 91–120 — Controlled Scale

- Expand winning product/audience combinations one at a time.
- Refresh creative based on qualified outcomes and message comprehension.
- Retire assets with misleading comments or poor-fit conversion even when click-through is high.
- Maintain support/review capacity ahead of spend.
- Introduce Mechanic Match, Seller Check, ClearSale, or Guided Service campaigns only after their separate release gates pass.

## 10. Weekly Operator Review

Every week record:

- Product availability and readiness label.
- Cases/applications by product and source.
- Review/support capacity and queue age.
- Safety escalations, complaints, corrections, privacy requests, and incidents.
- Message-understanding results.
- Creative/channel performance by qualified conversion.
- Evidence and permission status for potential stories.
- Decisions, owners, due dates, and stop conditions.

## 11. Founder Workload

The founder's recurring tasks should be limited to high-leverage responsibilities:

- Approve material product/business decisions.
- Recruit and communicate with the first testers.
- Participate in founder footage/interviews.
- Provide factual company history.
- Complete human-owned account, payment, contract, release, and partnership actions.
- Review launch scope, capacity, price, and public claims.

The operator should provide copy/paste instructions and prepared materials for each founder action.
