# Drivable Launch FAQ and Spokesperson Truth Guide V1

Status: Complete internal source copy. Public use remains blocked by launch approval and naming review.

## Approved description

Drivable by Mechanic’s Eye is being built to help people organize vehicle concerns and evidence into a clearer next step. Its intended evidence chain can support an owner concern, Buyer Check, seller disclosure, human review, and mechanic handoff without silently changing who supplied each fact.

Drivable does not replace a physical inspection, qualified technician, emergency services, or legal advice. It does not guarantee vehicle safety, diagnostic accuracy, seller honesty, or mechanic acceptance of a suggested cause.

## Public FAQ

### What is Drivable?

Drivable is a vehicle-evidence and decision-support product under development. It is designed to help a person describe a concern, collect available evidence, see bounded possible causes or risks, and prepare a clearer human or mechanic handoff.

### Is Drivable a diagnostic machine?

Not in the sense of a shop diagnostic procedure. Drivable may organize reported symptoms and supported evidence and rank up to three possible causes. Those are possibilities, not confirmed repairs or safety findings. A qualified technician may need to inspect and test the vehicle.

### Can it tell me whether my car is safe to drive?

No. Drivable must not certify safety. If there is smoke, fire, fuel odor, loss of braking or steering, severe overheating, sudden loss of power, an unsafe tire condition, or another immediate danger, stop using the vehicle when safe and contact emergency or roadside help as appropriate.

### What is Drivable Check?

The owner-focused experience is intended to organize vehicle context, driver observations, available codes or evidence, possible causes, uncertainty, missing evidence, and practical next steps.

### What is Buyer Check?

Buyer Check is intended to distinguish supported positives, observed concerns, seller statements, and unknowns. It is not a substitute for title research, a history report, a test drive, or an independent pre-purchase inspection, and it cannot prove the absence of hidden defects, undisclosed history, future failures, or fraud.

### What is the seller-disclosure experience?

The planned seller experience is designed to preserve seller statements as seller claims and keep them separate from observations, documents, test results, and reviewer findings. The public name “ClearSale” is provisional pending owner and legal review.

### Does Drivable guarantee honest listings?

No. Drivable can make evidence sources and unknowns more visible, but cannot guarantee that every participant is truthful or every defect has been found.

### Does AI turn a seller statement into a fact?

No. A seller statement remains a seller statement unless separate evidence supports it. Rewording, summarizing, or displaying it does not verify it. Public listings must identify AI-assisted wording and retain each claim’s source classification.

### What is Mechanic Match?

The current concept is request and referral support. It must not be described as live booking, guaranteed availability, certified matching, or a vetted network until those capabilities and standards exist and are documented.

### Does Drivable analyze photos, audio, video, or vibration?

Marketing may describe only media whose actual bytes are accepted, handled under the disclosed policy, and analyzed by a capable service in the promoted release. Until end-to-end proof exists, Drivable must not claim photo, audio, video, vision, or vibration analysis. Device vibration must never be presented as automotive vibration instrumentation.

### What does confidence mean?

Confidence describes how strongly available evidence supports a bounded possibility. It is not a repair-success probability, warranty, or safety rating. Missing, contradictory, poor-quality, or unverified evidence must lower confidence or prevent a conclusion.

### Why might Drivable ask for more evidence?

An honest unknown is safer than an invented conclusion. Drivable should identify what is missing and suggest the next reasonable observation, document, scan, test, human review, or professional inspection.

### Can a report change?

Yes. Reports should be versioned when new evidence, corrections, or reviewer findings arrive. A later finding must not rewrite history as though it was known earlier.

### Who owns evidence and how long is it kept?

The production answer is not sufficiently defined for a large public launch. Exact retention, deletion, export, provider, consent-version, and learning-use policies require owner/legal approval and working controls. Marketing must not promise permanent storage or durable reuse before then.

### Is information sold or used to train AI?

No answer beyond the approved Privacy Policy may be invented. Before launch, policy and controls must state the actual providers, purposes, sharing, retention, deletion/export process, and whether separate consent applies to learning use.

### Is Drivable available now?

The official website is available as a product reference, but coordinated public opening remains on hold until reliability, claims, policy, support, naming, and safety gates pass. Marketing must not imply broad production readiness before that decision.

### What does it cost?

No public price is approved in this documentation. Spokespeople must direct people to the official website after pricing is published and must not invent discounts, free periods, guarantees, or future pricing.

### Where can I learn more?

Use the official first-party site: https://www.getdrivable.com/.

## Spokesperson truth rules

1. Say “possible causes,” not “the diagnosis,” unless qualified human work confirms it.
2. Say “evidence-supported,” not “verified,” unless the verification method and result are named.
3. Attribute material claims as owner-reported, seller-stated, observed, document-derived, scan-derived, AI-inferred, or human-reviewed.
4. Never say safe, roadworthy, fraud-free, guaranteed honest, certified, vetted, first, only, or fully accurate without approved substantiation for that exact context.
5. Never infer media analysis from a visible upload control. Confirm byte receipt, capable analysis, provenance, retention, and release-level testing.
6. Treat missing evidence as a limitation, not permission to make a smoother claim.
7. Do not announce dates, prices, partnerships, customer counts, performance statistics, waitlist size, or success rates without a dated approved source.
8. Use “ClearSale” only as a provisional internal reference until an owner/counsel naming decision is recorded.
9. Correct misleading public statements promptly, preserve the prior version internally, and use the incident message library when material.

## Interview bridge language

- “What we can responsibly say today is that Drivable is designed to preserve the source of vehicle information and show what remains unknown.”
- “That feature is part of the product direction, but it is not a capability we are claiming for the current release.”
- “A ranked possibility is not a confirmed diagnosis; a technician may still need to inspect or test the vehicle.”
- “We do not turn a seller’s statement into a fact. It remains attributed unless separate evidence supports it.”
- “We are not making a safety determination from the app.”

## Mandatory escalation

Do not improvise answers about an injury, crash, alleged fraud, data exposure, regulator inquiry, threatened litigation, trademark dispute, individual customer evidence, law-enforcement request, partner dispute, or unreleased financial metric. Preserve the request and route it to the incident lead and appropriate owner-approved adviser.
