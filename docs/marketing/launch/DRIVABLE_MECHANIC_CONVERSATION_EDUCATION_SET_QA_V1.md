## Complete organic captions

### Card 1

Clear questions can make a repair conversation easier to understand. Ask what was observed, measured, tested, or ruled out; which work is urgent and why; what the price includes; and whether the shop will call before the authorized total changes.

No phrase alone proves that a mechanic is dishonest. The goal is a clearer shared record, not an accusation.

Educational information only. Not a diagnosis, repair recommendation, price estimate, or vehicle-safety determination.

### Card 2

A symptom or diagnostic code can support more than one possible cause. Before replacing a part, a useful question is: “What test would distinguish these causes?”

That question does not challenge a professional's integrity. It asks for the decision path connecting the evidence to the proposed work.

Educational information only. Not repair advice or a diagnosis.

### Card 3

Try separating four things in a repair conversation:

1. What was observed?
2. What was measured?
3. How was it interpreted?
4. What remains unknown?

Clear labels make it easier to understand why work is recommended and which uncertainty remains. Missing detail alone does not prove wrongdoing.

Educational information only. Not a diagnosis, inspection, or safety determination.

### Card 4

Before authorizing work, ask what happens if the first test does not support the suspected cause. What evidence would come next? At what point would the shop request more time, money, testing, or authorization?

A clear decision path can reduce misunderstanding. It does not guarantee cost, timing, diagnosis, repair success, or outcome.

Educational information only. Not repair advice.

## Safety and fairness review

- None of the cards claims a diagnosis or tells a user which part to replace.
- None implies that a particular mechanic, shop, estimate, phrase, or recommendation is dishonest.
- The set explicitly says clear questions are not accusations and missing information alone is not proof of wrongdoing.
- No savings, price, timing, repair-success, credential, warranty, or safety promise appears.
- No user is encouraged to inspect a moving or unsafe vehicle, handle tools, or collect evidence while driving.
- The cards support a bounded conversation and authorization process; they do not replace a qualified inspection, diagnosis, estimate, or service agreement.
