# Drivable Before/After Scenarios V1

## Purpose

Show the value of a clearer next move without implying guaranteed diagnosis, safety, repair, purchase, or sale outcomes.

## 1. Brake Vibration

**Before:**
The customer feels steering-wheel vibration while braking and is scared the brakes may fail. A shop mentioned pads, but no inspection details were explained.

**Drivable process:**
They submit vehicle details, when the vibration occurs, pedal feel, warning lights, recent work, and safe tire/dashboard photos.

**After:**
They receive possible brake, wheel, tire, bearing, suspension, and ABS paths; moderate confidence; stop-now warning signs; missing evidence; and a script asking the shop what was measured and inspected. The vehicle is not declared safe to drive.

## 2. Overheating

**Before:**
The temperature gauge rose and the customer does not know whether to keep driving home.

**Drivable process:**
From a safe stopped location, they submit the gauge behavior, warning messages, steam or smell observations, recent coolant work, and a safe dashboard photo.

**After:**
They receive conservative guidance to keep the vehicle stopped when overheating risk is present, avoid opening a hot pressurized system, seek qualified help, and document what a shop later finds.

## 3. No-Start

**Before:**
The vehicle clicks but will not start. The customer is ready to buy a battery without knowing whether the battery is the cause.

**Drivable process:**
They submit battery age, light behavior, click pattern, recent jump-start result, weather, and safe audio/video.

**After:**
They get possible battery, connection, starter, charging, key/security, or engine paths; confidence limits; safe checks; and a request for battery/charging test results before buying parts.

## 4. Check Engine Light

**Before:**
The check engine light appears and the customer assumes the engine is ruined.

**Drivable process:**
They submit whether the light is steady or flashing, symptoms, recent fueling or repair events, scan codes if available, and a dashboard photo.

**After:**
They receive a risk-aware explanation, possible paths tied to the code and symptoms, missing evidence, and instructions to seek prompt in-person help when the light flashes or drivability changes.

## 5. Used Car Remote Buy

**Before:**
A buyer finds a low-priced vehicle several hours away and is tempted to send a deposit.

**Drivable process:**
They submit the listing, seller claims, VIN if voluntarily available, photos, records, title statements, disclosed issues, and inspection access.

**After:**
They get missing-proof questions, red flags, inspection priorities, and confidence-rated pursue, pause, inspect, or walk-away guidance. They are reminded that title, ownership, mileage, and condition are not verified.

## 6. As-Is Seller

**Before:**
The seller knows the vehicle has problems but does not know how to describe them without sounding misleading.

**Drivable process:**
They submit known symptoms, repair history, title status, runs-and-drives status, available photos, and what remains unknown.

**After:**
They receive an organized condition summary, evidence checklist, buyer questions to expect, and disclosure-oriented draft language for seller review. The vehicle is not certified or guaranteed.

## 7. Transmission Slipping

**Before:**
The engine revs during shifts and the customer is unsure whether to approve an expensive transmission replacement.

**Drivable process:**
They submit when it slips, temperature, speed/load, warning lights, fluid observations when safely available, scan codes, service history, and estimate details.

**After:**
They receive possible fluid, control, sensor, solenoid, internal wear, or related engine paths; low-to-moderate confidence until testing; and questions about codes, pressure tests, fluid condition, and verified failure evidence.

## 8. Electrical Issue

**Before:**
Lights flicker, the battery dies intermittently, and different people suggest battery, alternator, or wiring.

**Drivable process:**
They submit battery/alternator test results, start behavior, warning lights, recent battery work, accessory symptoms, weather, and safe photos or video.

**After:**
They get organized battery, connection, charging, parasitic-draw, ground, fuse, module, or wiring paths and a testing sequence to discuss with a qualified technician.

## 9. Weird Noise

**Before:**
The customer says, "It makes a weird noise," and cannot get a useful answer online.

**Drivable process:**
They safely record the noise while parked when possible and describe location, timing, engine speed, vehicle speed, temperature, turning, braking, and recent work.

**After:**
They receive a clearer symptom summary, possible noise families, confidence limits, stop-now signs, and a mechanic script that explains exactly when the sound occurs.

## 10. Repair vs Sell Decision

**Before:**
An older vehicle needs another repair. The customer is emotionally and financially stuck between fixing it and selling it.

**Drivable process:**
They submit vehicle goals, mileage, condition, known issues, repair estimate, recent repair spending, reliability needs, and available evidence.

**After:**
They receive professional-repair, staged-diagnosis, monitor, sell-as-is, and walk-away paths with tradeoffs and missing facts. Drivable does not guarantee future reliability, sale price, or the best financial outcome.
