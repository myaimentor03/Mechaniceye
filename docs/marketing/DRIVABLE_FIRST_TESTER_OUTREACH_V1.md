# Drivable First Tester Outreach V1

## Purpose

Help Glenn recruit a small number of useful early testers after the complete Drivable flow is proven with a Glenn-owned identity and test inbox.

The first tester round is product validation, not a public launch. Drivable provides informational, confidence-rated guidance based on information provided. It is not a certified inspection, safety clearance, confirmed diagnosis, or guarantee.

## Launch Gate Before Outreach

Do not invite an external tester until:

- The intended intake route has a documented final `PASS`.
- Raw JSON, the AI draft, the reviewed recommendation, and the resulting record can be traced.
- Admin email reaches a monitored Glenn-owned inbox.
- Customer email is disabled, draft-only, or deliberately tested with a Glenn-owned inbox.
- Human review and the send-safety gate work.
- The tester can receive a reviewed report without exposing another customer's data.
- Glenn has a way to capture what the tester did and what actually happened.

## Who to Ask First

Start with people who:

- Know and trust Glenn.
- Will say when something is confusing.
- Understand this is an early informational product.
- Have a real but non-emergency vehicle decision.
- Can provide clear vehicle details and safe evidence.
- Will complete a short follow-up after receiving guidance.
- Will not treat a preview as permission to drive or skip inspection.

Good first candidates:

1. A friend or family member with a mild, stable vehicle concern.
2. A local car person willing to critique the questions and report.
3. A used-car buyer comparing whether a listing is worth pursuing.
4. An owner deciding between another repair and selling as-is.
5. A seller who needs help explaining known issues honestly.

## Who Not to Ask Yet

Do not use early testing for:

- Active emergencies, crashes, fire, fuel leaks, severe overheating, brake failure, major steering failure, or dangerous roadside exposure.
- People who need towing, repair, legal, title, or purchase certainty immediately.
- Strangers expecting a guaranteed diagnosis or instant response.
- Customers with disputes, fraud claims, insurance claims, or litigation.
- High-value purchases where no independent inspection is planned.
- Anyone unwilling to provide feedback or respect safety limitations.
- Paid public traffic before the end-to-end flow is proven.

## Message for a Friend or Family Tester

> I am testing an early vehicle decision tool called Drivable. It helps organize symptoms, evidence, possible paths, confidence, and the next useful step. It does not replace a mechanic or guarantee a diagnosis. If you have a real, non-emergency car question, would you try the flow and tell me what was confusing or useful? I would also ask what you did afterward so I can test the follow-up process.

## Message for a Local Car Person

> I am testing Drivable, an early confidence-rated vehicle guidance product. I need someone who knows cars to pressure-test the intake questions, evidence requests, uncertainty language, mechanic script, and safety escalation. This is not asking you to endorse a diagnosis. I want direct feedback on where the product overreaches, misses evidence, or fails to give a practical next move.

## Message for a Used Car Buyer

> Looking at a used car and not sure whether it is worth chasing? I am testing Drivable's remote buyer-risk flow. You submit the listing, seller claims, photos, records, and your questions. Drivable organizes red flags, missing proof, confidence, and what to ask or inspect next. It is not a pre-purchase inspection and does not verify title, ownership, mileage, seller claims, or condition. Would you test it before spending time or money on the vehicle?

## Message for Someone With a Broken Car

> I am testing an early tool for people who are unsure what to do next with a vehicle problem. You describe the symptoms and safely share useful photos, video, audio, or scan information. Drivable returns possible paths, confidence, missing evidence, and questions for a shop. It cannot confirm the cause or say a vehicle is safe to drive. If your issue is stable and not an emergency, would you test the flow and follow up with what the shop actually found?

## Message for Someone Selling a Car As-Is

> I am testing a Drivable seller flow that helps organize known issues, repair history, evidence, and clearer as-is listing language. It does not inspect, certify, sell, title, or guarantee the vehicle. The seller remains responsible for truthful disclosure and the transaction. Would you test whether the flow helps you explain the vehicle more clearly and identify missing information buyers may ask for?

## What Glenn Should Ask Them to Submit

Ask only for information needed for the selected test:

- Vehicle year, make, model, trim, and approximate mileage.
- The decision they are trying to make.
- Symptoms or concerns in their own words.
- When the issue happens and whether it is changing.
- Warning lights, smells, leaks, smoke, noises, vibration, or loss of power.
- Recent repairs, events, or seller claims.
- Safe photos, video, audio, scan screenshots, records, or listing screenshots.
- What they already checked.
- What outcome would make the guidance useful.

Remind testers:

- Do not drive, recreate a symptom, crawl under a vehicle, touch hot parts, or open a pressurized system for evidence.
- Remove unrelated personal information from uploads.
- Skip anything unsafe or sensitive.

## What Glenn Should Watch During the Test

- Can the tester choose the right starting path without help?
- Do they understand what information is being requested and why?
- Do they know which evidence is optional?
- Does any wording sound like a diagnosis, guarantee, or safety clearance?
- Are safety-critical symptoms escalated early enough?
- Does the report separate observations, possible causes, missing evidence, and recommendations?
- Is confidence understandable?
- Does the tester know the next move after reading the report?
- Does the mechanic script feel useful and accurate?
- Are internal notes and review controls kept out of customer views?
- Does the correct route, record, review status, and recipient appear?
- How long does manual review and fulfillment take?
- Where does Glenn need to explain the product verbally?

## Follow-Up Questions After the Test

1. What decision were you trying to make?
2. Where did you hesitate or get confused?
3. Which question or evidence request felt unnecessary?
4. Which part of the report was most useful?
5. Did the confidence rating make sense?
6. Did anything sound too certain or too reassuring?
7. Did the safety language change what you did?
8. What action did you take?
9. What did a mechanic, inspection, seller, or buyer later confirm?
10. What repair or outcome occurred, and what did it cost if you are comfortable sharing?
11. Did the guidance help: yes, no, or unknown?
12. What would have made the next move clearer?
13. Would you have paid for this? Which report and at what price?
14. Would you recommend it to someone with a similar decision?
