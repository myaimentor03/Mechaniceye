# Drivable Local Launch Plan V1

## Purpose

Stage a controlled local launch that proves usefulness, safety, fulfillment, and outcome capture before Drivable accepts broad or paid public traffic.

## Readiness Rule

The sequence is:

1. Glenn-owned end-to-end test.
2. One trusted external tester.
3. First 10 controlled testers.
4. First 25 testers.
5. First 50 testers.
6. Paid testing only after the relevant flow repeatedly works.

Pause whenever route, recipient, review, safety, privacy, or follow-up behavior is uncertain.

## First 10 Testers

### Goal

Find basic product failures while Glenn can personally observe every case.

### Mix

- 3 owners with stable current problems.
- 2 people making repair-versus-sell decisions.
- 2 used-car buyers.
- 1 as-is seller.
- 1 local car person or mechanic-adjacent reviewer.
- 1 sitting or no-start vehicle owner.

### Operating Method

- Invite one person at a time.
- Review every intake and customer-facing message manually.
- Keep customer email controlled.
- Record time spent from intake through delivery.
- Complete outcome follow-up for every case.
- Fix serious confusion before inviting the next person.

### Success Gate

- Correct route and record for all completed tests.
- No unintended customer sends.
- No unsafe certainty.
- At least 7 of 10 testers understand the next move without a call.
- Outcome or meaningful follow-up captured for at least 7 of 10.

## First 25 Testers

### Goal

Test repeatability across scenarios and reduce Glenn's need to explain the product.

### Add

- More roadside uncertainty without active emergencies.
- Dashboard-light and weird-noise cases.
- More buyer listing reviews.
- More seller-as-is cases.
- At least 3 testers outside Glenn's close personal circle.

### Operating Method

- Use a simple tester tracker.
- Group confusion by intake, evidence, report, email, safety, or follow-up.
- Track which cases need human escalation.
- Compare expected report scope with actual fulfillment time.
- Test a small number of comped offer labels without payment integration.

### Success Gate

- At least 80% complete the tested flow without direct intervention.
- Safety escalations are appropriate and documented.
- Most reports require editing for judgment, not basic formatting repair.
- Follow-up can distinguish customer-reported and verified outcomes.
- No repeated critical issue remains open.

## First 50 Testers

### Goal

Determine whether a narrow local paid pilot is supportable.

### Add

- Controlled community-group testers.
- Buyer and seller groups.
- Referrals from local mechanics, mobile mechanics, inspectors, or detailers.
- A balanced mix of First Look, Full Decision, buyer, and seller cases.

### Operating Method

- Cap weekly volume to available review capacity.
- Publish clear eligibility and response expectations.
- Keep high-risk cases in human review.
- Measure conversion interest without promising instant service.
- Use structured outcome statuses and weekly quality review.

### Success Gate

- Stable route, review, delivery, and follow-up performance.
- Clear repeat demand for at least one offer.
- Fulfillment time supports the proposed price range.
- Refund, correction, and support policies are documented.
- Privacy, consent, terms, and payment decisions are ready for a paid pilot.

## Where to Find Testers Locally

- Friends, family, coworkers, and neighbors.
- Local Facebook neighborhood groups.
- Local car enthusiast and make/model groups.
- Community college automotive programs.
- Local mobile mechanics and independent shops.
- Used-car inspection services.
- Detailers, tow operators, tire shops, and parts-store communities.
- Apartment, workplace, church, and neighborhood networks.
- Local private-party buyer and seller communities.

Use direct moderator permission when a group prohibits promotion or research requests.

## Facebook Groups and Community Posts

Suggested post:

> Local vehicle owners and used-car shoppers: I am testing an early tool called Drivable. It helps organize symptoms, evidence, possible paths, confidence, and the next useful questions before a repair, purchase, sale, or tow decision. It is informational and does not replace an inspection or guarantee a diagnosis. I am looking for a small number of local testers with real, non-emergency vehicle decisions who will give honest feedback and report what happened afterward. Comment or message me if that fits.

Do not:

- Post as emergency help.
- Claim AI diagnoses vehicles.
- Offer safety clearance.
- Imply a mechanic or shop partnership that does not exist.
- Collect sensitive records publicly in comments.

## Used Car Buyer and Seller Groups

Buyer angle:

- Review the listing before traveling or paying for an inspection.
- Identify seller questions, missing proof, and inspection priorities.
- Never claim title, ownership, mileage, or condition is verified.

Seller angle:

- Organize known issues and repair history.
- Improve clarity before writing an as-is listing.
- Never claim the vehicle is certified, approved, or guaranteed.

## Mechanic and Referral Angle

Position Drivable as preparation, not competition:

- Better symptom summaries.
- More useful evidence before an appointment.
- Clearer customer questions.
- A mechanic script that asks what was tested before parts are approved.
- Earlier routing to in-person help when risk or evidence requires it.

Ask referral partners:

- Which intake details reduce wasted time?
- Which evidence is useful versus misleading?
- Which cases should never be handled remotely?
- Can they share the confirmed outcome with customer consent?

Do not promise referrals, bookings, lead quality, repair volume, or exclusivity.

## What Not to Promise

- A confirmed diagnosis.
- That a vehicle is safe to drive.
- That a customer should drive before inspection.
- A tow or no-tow guarantee.
- A repair price or successful repair.
- A sale, offer, buyer, title result, or transaction.
- Instant human review or a fixed response time unless operations can meet it.
- That the system automatically learns from every case.
- That AI replaces a mechanic or inspection.

## When to Charge

Do not charge public testers until:

- The selected flow passes end to end repeatedly.
- The offer and included deliverable are clear.
- Glenn knows average fulfillment time.
- Human review capacity is defined.
- Payment verification, refund, correction, and support handling are documented.
- The customer can understand limitations before paying.
- Outcome follow-up is operational.

Begin with a narrow paid pilot, one offer, and capped volume. The `First Look Report` is the simplest starting candidate. Planning prices remain placeholders until tested.

## What to Measure

- Invites sent and testers started.
- Completion rate by scenario.
- Drop-off step.
- Missing-information rate.
- Human-review rate.
- Safety escalation rate.
- Report delivery time.
- Glenn's review minutes per case.
- Customer understanding of confidence.
- Next-action clarity.
- Customer action taken.
- Actual outcome captured.
- Advice helped: `yes`, `no`, or `unknown`.
- Willingness to pay and preferred offer.
- Corrections, complaints, refunds, or privacy requests.
- Referral intent.

## When to Stop and Fix the Product

Pause new testers when:

- A route misfires or loses data.
- A real or unintended recipient receives test email.
- A high-risk case bypasses human review.
- The product implies safety clearance or diagnostic certainty.
- Testers repeatedly choose the wrong path.
- Required evidence is confusing or encourages risky behavior.
- Reports do not produce a clear next move.
- Different customers' information could be mixed.
- Glenn cannot keep up with review or follow-up.
- A repeated issue affects more than 10% of recent tests.
- A tester reports harm, near harm, or a serious misunderstanding.

Record the issue, preserve evidence, correct it, rerun controlled tests, and only then resume.
