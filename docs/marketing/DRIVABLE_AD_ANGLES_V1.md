# Drivable Ad Angles V1

## Use Rules

- Use only after the relevant product flow is ready for the intended audience.
- Never imply a confirmed diagnosis, safety clearance, guaranteed repair, or guaranteed transaction.
- Pair urgent symptoms with stop-and-seek-help language.
- Describe Drivable as informational, confidence-rated guidance based on information provided.

## 1. Stranded or Worried Driver

**Headline:** Stuck with a car problem and not sure what comes next?

**Short hook:** Organize the symptoms, warning lights, and safe evidence before deciding whether to stop, call for help, or ask a shop.

**Target customer:** A worried driver who has already reached a safe place.

**CTA:** Get a clearer next move

**Safety/trust note:** Drivable does not provide emergency dispatch or declare a vehicle safe to drive. Critical symptoms require qualified in-person help.

## 2. Repair vs Sell

**Headline:** One more repair - or time to sell it as-is?

**Short hook:** Compare the evidence, likely cost paths, vehicle goals, and what is still unknown.

**Target customer:** An owner facing another estimate or recurring problem.

**CTA:** Compare my options

**Safety/trust note:** Guidance is informational and cannot guarantee repair cost, sale value, buyer interest, or financial outcome.

## 3. Used Car Buyer

**Headline:** Before you chase that used car, find out what is missing.

**Short hook:** Turn a listing, seller claims, photos, and records into better questions and inspection priorities.

**Target customer:** A private-party or remote used-car buyer.

**CTA:** Review the listing first

**Safety/trust note:** This is not a pre-purchase inspection and does not verify title, ownership, mileage, seller claims, or condition.

## 4. As-Is Seller

**Headline:** Selling a problem car? Explain it clearly.

**Short hook:** Organize known issues, repair history, evidence, and the questions buyers are likely to ask.

**Target customer:** A private seller listing a vehicle as-is.

**CTA:** Prepare my as-is listing

**Safety/trust note:** Drivable does not inspect, certify, own, sell, title, finance, or guarantee the vehicle. Sellers remain responsible for truthful disclosures.

## 5. Dashboard Light Panic

**Headline:** A warning light came on. Do not let panic make the decision.

**Short hook:** Capture the exact light, symptoms, timing, and available codes for a confidence-rated next-step review.

**Target customer:** A driver seeing a new dashboard warning.

**CTA:** Organize what happened

**Safety/trust note:** Red brake, oil-pressure, overheating, smoke, fire, or severe drivability warnings may require stopping and immediate in-person help.

## 6. Weird Noise

**Headline:** That weird noise is hard to explain. Start with evidence.

**Short hook:** Share when it happens, where it seems to come from, and a safe audio or video clip.

**Target customer:** An owner dealing with a knock, squeal, rattle, hum, or grind.

**CTA:** Build a clearer symptom report

**Safety/trust note:** Do not drive or recreate a symptom to record it. Remote audio cannot confirm the cause.

## 7. Vibration

**Headline:** Vibration at speed, under braking, or at idle?

**Short hook:** The timing matters. Drivable helps separate what you feel from what still needs inspection.

**Target customer:** An owner with steering, braking, wheel, engine, or driveline vibration.

**CTA:** Map the vibration clues

**Safety/trust note:** Severe shaking, braking changes, steering changes, or wheel/tire concerns require prompt in-person help.

## 8. Before Buying a Cheap Car

**Headline:** The cheapest car can become the most expensive decision.

**Short hook:** Check the listing, known issues, missing records, seller answers, and inspection access before you spend more.

**Target customer:** A budget buyer considering a low-priced vehicle.

**CTA:** Check before you chase it

**Safety/trust note:** Drivable cannot verify the vehicle or transaction. Use an independent inspection and verify ownership/title before paying.

## 9. Before Wasting Repair Money

**Headline:** Before approving parts, ask what the evidence supports.

**Short hook:** Get possible paths, missing checks, confidence, and questions for the shop.

**Target customer:** An owner facing a repair estimate or parts recommendation.

**CTA:** Review the next repair decision

**Safety/trust note:** Drivable does not overrule a qualified inspection or guarantee a cheaper or successful repair.

## 10. Ask Before You Tow

**Headline:** Ask before you tow - but do not delay when risk is high.

**Short hook:** If you are already in a safe place, organize the symptoms and risk signals before choosing the next call.

**Target customer:** A stopped driver unsure whether roadside help, towing, or inspection is appropriate.

**CTA:** Review the roadside situation

**Safety/trust note:** Drivable is not emergency dispatch and cannot guarantee tow/no-tow decisions. Dangerous symptoms or locations require immediate qualified help.

## 11. What Should I Tell the Mechanic?

**Headline:** Walk into the shop with a better story.

**Short hook:** Turn scattered symptoms into a mechanic script: what happens, when, what changed, and what you want verified.

**Target customer:** An owner preparing for a shop call or appointment.

**CTA:** Build my mechanic script

**Safety/trust note:** The script supports communication; it does not diagnose the vehicle or dictate repairs.

## 12. Is This Worth Fixing?

**Headline:** Is this vehicle worth fixing - for you?

**Short hook:** Compare the problem, evidence, likely decision paths, vehicle goals, and what needs inspection before committing.

**Target customer:** An owner balancing cost, reliability, time, and vehicle value.

**CTA:** See the decision paths

**Safety/trust note:** Drivable cannot guarantee repair cost, future reliability, resale value, or the best financial choice.

## Optional Testing Variations

For each angle, test:

- Problem-first versus decision-first headline.
- "Get a clearer next move" versus scenario-specific CTA.
- Static image versus 15-second video.
- Customer-facing trust note in caption versus on-screen footer.
- Local tester invitation versus paid-report interest.

Measure qualified starts, completed intakes, report understanding, safety escalations, and outcome follow-up - not clicks alone.
