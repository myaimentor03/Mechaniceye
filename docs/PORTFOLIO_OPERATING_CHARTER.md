# Portfolio Operating Charter

## North Star
Build owner-controlled, increasingly autonomous businesses that produce durable cash flow and enterprise value while minimizing required owner labor and avoiding existential risk.

## Priority Rule
Drivable remains Company #1 and the dominant operating priority until one of the following is objectively true:
1. Drivable is financially stable enough to run with bounded owner involvement; or
2. Another business has proven, with measured revenue and economics, that it deserves greater capital allocation.

No new venture may materially slow the November 2, 2026 paid-beta objective.

## Portfolio Doctrine
The holding-company system is a capital-allocation machine, not a collection of hobbies. Every business, agent, machine, service, and dollar must do at least one of the following:
- increase recurring cash flow;
- increase proprietary data or intellectual property;
- increase distribution or customer acquisition capability;
- reduce operating risk;
- reduce required owner labor; or
- cheaply validate an opportunity with asymmetric upside.

## Capital Model
External capital target: up to $10,000 every three months until the portfolio becomes self-financing.

Businesses earn capital through evidence. Capital is not allocated merely because an idea is interesting.

Required progression:
IDEA -> RESEARCH -> DEMAND SIGNAL -> LOW-COST VALIDATION -> FIRST REVENUE -> REPEATABLE REVENUE -> SCALE

Kill weak experiments early. Concentrate capital behind measured winners.

## Business Types
Favor a balanced portfolio of:
1. Cash Engines: fast-to-revenue, AI-assisted services and other low-capital businesses.
2. Scalable Software: recurring-revenue software/data products, including Drivable.
3. Asymmetric Experiments: very low-cost tests with large possible upside.

Prefer digital delivery, recurring revenue, high gross margin, low inventory, measurable online distribution, and work that AI can perform substantially.

## Six-Station Infrastructure Model
1. Drivable Production: current product, launch, reliability, customer issues, releases.
2. Drivable R&D: future capabilities, healthy baselines, diagnosis improvements, experiments.
3. Venture Laboratory: market discovery, validation, new-company experiments.
4. Revenue Factory: sales, lead generation, content, SEO, conversion, reactivation, analytics.
5. Corporate Headquarters: Paperclip/control plane, budgets, KPIs, governance, finance preparation, portfolio management.
6. Infrastructure / QA: GitHub runners, testing, monitoring, backups, networking, deployments, databases.

Machines are worker capacity, not permanent silos. Capacity may be reassigned according to portfolio priorities.

## Governance
The human owner retains final authority over ownership, sale of the parent company, major debt, equity issuance, major capital transfers, major legal commitments, changes to this charter, and other reserved matters.

AI executives operate with bounded authority, budgets, audit trails, approval thresholds, and automatic hard stops.

Illustrative authority ladder:
- small reversible experiment: autonomous within approved budget;
- moderate spend or material operational change: executive approval under predefined rules;
- major spend, binding legal obligation, ownership change, major debt, or high-risk action: owner approval required.

## Executive Hierarchy
Target operating structure:
- Portfolio CEO
- CFO
- CTO
- Chief Revenue Officer
- Chief Operations Officer
- Chief Risk Officer
- Venture Director
- Research Director
- Infrastructure Director

Each executive owns measurable goals, budgets, workers, KPIs, and escalation rules.

## Autonomous Venture Factory
Continuously discover opportunities, but build only after evidence.

Default funnel:
100 ideas discovered -> 25 researched -> 10 scored -> 3 validated -> 1 receives an MVP or funded test.

Most ideas should die cheaply. Failure to kill weak ideas is itself a portfolio failure.

## Shared Services
Build reusable capabilities once and share them across businesses:
- customer acquisition knowledge;
- pricing and conversion data;
- sales systems;
- content and SEO operations;
- accounting and financial reporting preparation;
- infrastructure and deployment;
- security and audit;
- agent-performance metrics;
- business-launch templates.

## Technology Principle
Exploit free and low-cost resources first when they are reliable enough: recovered hardware, GitHub-hosted and self-hosted runners, free model capacity, Codespaces as burst capacity, Oracle or similar free compute when available, secure private networking, and lightweight serverless automation.

Pay for infrastructure when paid capacity produces more expected value than the best viable free fallback.

## Data Moat
Each company should deliberately accumulate proprietary operating intelligence. Drivable's moat includes vehicle, symptom, evidence, diagnosis, repair, cost, resolution, sale, buyer outcome, confidence, escalation, and healthy-baseline data. Other companies should preserve their equivalent acquisition, pricing, conversion, operational, and outcome data.

## Owner-Light Target
Engineering objective: within roughly one year, move the owner from daily operator toward controlling owner/chairman whose attention is primarily reserved for exceptions, strategy, capital, governance, and family priorities.

This is an operating target, not a guaranteed financial outcome.

## Self-Financing Milestone
The portfolio reaches a critical transition when operating cash flow can fund:
- operating expenses;
- owner distributions;
- retained reserves; and
- new experiments
without requiring the next external $10,000 infusion.

At that point, outside capital becomes optional acceleration rather than life support.

## Permanent Anti-Drift Rule
Do not sacrifice a proven asset for an unproven idea. Do not let agent activity substitute for business results. Do not scale before evidence. Do not allow infrastructure ambition to outrun revenue. Do not touch production casually.

## Amendment Rule
This charter may evolve when measured evidence or a materially superior architecture emerges, but changes must preserve owner control, bounded AI authority, evidence-based capital allocation, and the priority of proven cash-generating assets.
